## 9. Key Artifacts and Mechanics

### The Dreamstone `[LOCKED — substantially expanded v0.9.4]`

A source of resonance power — but per v0.9.4 cosmology, Dreamstones are now precisely understood:

**What Dreamstones are `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`:**

- **A unique stone material** that can *store memories and emotional energy*. The same material the four original tribal artifacts that became the Treasures of Ruin were made from (Section 2 — Cormorian founding myth).
- **Natural Dreamstones accumulate *positive* emotional and memory energy** across millennia
- **Inverted Dreamstones (the Treasures of Ruin) accumulate *negative* emotional and memory energy** — same material, *opposite polarity*

**The polarity framework `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`:**

| Polarity | Form | Energy Source | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Natural Dreamstones | Accumulated positive memories/emotions across millennia | The Dreamstone the player encounters; the Royal Palace Dreamstone; Slither Wing encounter site; Mt. Silver encounter (with Keerin) |
| **Negative** | Inverted Dreamstones | Accumulated negative memories/emotions / Phancero's residual contamination | The four Treasures of Ruin (Wo-Chien, Chien-Pao, Ting-Lu, Chi-Yu) — *same material*, *flipped polarity* |

**This explains why Dreamstones resonate with Treasures and serve as incarnation catalysts** — they are the same material at different polarities. The cults use positive Dreamstones to *re-align* the negative-polarity Treasures' resonance frequency, enabling material incarnation without drawing Arceus's intervention (Section 8 — Cult Cosmological Framework).

**Dreamstone capabilities `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`:**

- **Resonance catalysis** — can resonate with the four inverted Treasures, enabling their material incarnation
- **Time-displacement** — sufficiently powerful Dreamstones can open paths through time. **Confirmed in-project examples:**
  - The Slither Wing / Ancient Cormoria encounter (Section 9 — Ancient Cormoria flashback) — Reid + Sable + player are pulled into Ancient Cormoria via Dreamstone resonance
  - **Cormor's Avalon-rest** (Section 9 — Cormor's True Status) — Cormor and Keerin used a Dreamstone to open a path to the distant past where Cormor sleeps in dreamless rest
  - **The Mt. Silver / Future-Red encounter** (Section 7 — Future-Red; Section 9 — Keerin) — Keerin's pact-power transports the player across temporal-cosmological space to face Future-Red
- **Bloodmoon Ursaluna side quest** — used as a catalyst in the Teddiursa-pick post-Champion content
- **The protagonist's sealing ritual** — Osrid (with Ting-Lu) eventually uses the reassembled Mega Dreamstone's power to seal the Treasures back; the backlash costs him his memory (restored later in Paldea — Section 5 — Protagonist)

**Time flows asymmetrically across Dreamstone displacement** — ~12 subjective years pass in Ancient Cormoria for every ~10 real-world years. Sable Ashland's 12-year subjective stay in Ancient Cormoria during a 10-year present-time absence is the canonical example of this asymmetry.

### Dreamstone resonance — disruptor, accelerator, and the three-stone seal `[LOCKED v0.9.14]`

A Mega Dreamstone's resonance with a Treasure of Ruin can run in **either direction**, depending on configuration:

- **Accelerator (single stone):** one Dreamstone resonating with one Treasure *re-aligns* the Treasure's negative-polarity frequency and drives its material incarnation — the operation every cult is attempting (Section 8 — Cult Cosmological Framework).
- **Disruptor (competing stones):** if a *second* Dreamstone enters the resonance field while a Treasure is mid-ritual, the two stones destabilize the operation and can **shatter everything involved** — the Treasure and both Dreamstones. *(This is **not** what befell Alaric's ritual — that failure came from a **misaligned Leyline point**, not a competing stone — but the principle is why the cults keep their operations isolated and why the three-stone endgame below works.)*
- **The three-stone macroresonance seal — the endgame `[LOCKED v0.9.14]`:** bring **all three Mega Dreamstones into the same place as all four Treasures of Ruin**, and the compound resonance can **seal all four Treasures away completely** — and potentially unmake the root distortions themselves. This is **the same class of event Cormor used against Phancero** at the founding (Section 2 — Founding Myth): a calamity-scale macroresonance, here turned to *pacify* the Treasures rather than banish a distortion — suppressing their reality-warping capacity for (at least) a few thousand years.

**Roma's reconstruction `[LOCKED v0.9.14]`:** the 9 scattered shards are inert as a catalyst until reassembled. **Madame Roma** (Section 7 — Madame Roma) can **reconstruct the shattered Mt. Mirroh Mega Dreamstone from its 9 fragments**, drawing on a **blessing given to her by Keerin** (Keerin, below). This is the in-fiction mechanism behind the reassembled-Mega-Dreamstone catalyst — Osrid recovers the shards; Roma makes them whole.

**How the climax actually goes `[LOCKED v0.9.14]`:** despite Osrid and the empire's best efforts, the macroresonance seal at the Necropolis does **not** cleanly solve the problem. It seals the Treasures *to a degree*, but **releases far too much energy** — triggering a massive cascade of **earthquakes that permanently reshape the terrain of Cormoria** (the aged, altered land seen ~2,000 years later in *Dreamstone Mysteries*) — and the backlash **shocks Osrid**, wiping nearly all of his memory. His memory is restored only much later, in Paldea, when he reunites with Eden in a Pokémon battle (Section 5 — Protagonist).

### The Mega-Dreamstone Shard Quest — Parallel Main Quest `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`

**The single largest structural addition of v0.9.7.** Dreamstone Ruination's narrative spine is now a *double helix:* the public-facing Trial circuit and the clandestine Mega-Dreamstone shard hunt, running in parallel across all 9 chapters of the game leading to the Championship.

**The three Mega Dreamstones `[LOCKED v0.9.14 — expanded from the v0.9.7 "two Dreamstones" framing]`:**

Dreamstones are common across the wider world — most are *small fossils* that have absorbed hundreds of thousands of years of Pokémon and human memory since prehistory (the same material as above, at natural/positive polarity). But Cormoria's official history and mythology speak of **three *special* Mega Dreamstones** — the three stones **Cormor Dream-Eater, Keerin, and their companions used to seal away the calamity (Phancero)** before the empire was founded (Section 2 — Founding Myth). Only these three hold enough resonance to fully align with a Treasure of Ruin, which is exactly why the four Ruin Cults hunt them.

**None of the three locations were publicly known.** It took the Widening Eye — and then the successor cults after it split — **years** of effort to even narrow down where the stones might be. The three:

1. **The Silversun Mega Dreamstone** — sealed in **Silversun Palace**, in the same chamber where **Justice the Iron Valiant** (the royal family's ace) was uncovered (Section 7 — Royal Family; Justice, below). Held under the royal family's institutional protection. **No cult knows the royals hold it** — they have only *hypothesized* that a Mega Dreamstone lies in the capital (the Beads and Sword Cults *suspect* a palace Dreamstone exists but have not located it). This is the "palace Dreamstone" of prior canon.
2. **The Mt. Mirroh Mega Dreamstone → the 9 shards.** Found by **Harbinger Alaric** through sheer luck — the one time he ever beat Baradus and Goma to anything (Section 8 — Vessels Cult). In his impatience to incarnate Ting-Lu he chose a Mt. Mirroh Leyline point that was **not** perfectly aligned; though the incarnation "succeeded," the misaligned resonance **shattered the Mega Dreamstone into 9 fragments**, scattered across Cormoria through **miniature interdimensional rifts** torn into space-time by the force of Ting-Lu's incarnation. These 9 shards are the object of the parallel main quest (below), hunted by Osrid *and* by both surviving cults. **This resolves the prior `[OPEN]` shatter-cause: it was Alaric's misaligned Mt. Mirroh incarnation.**
3. **The Necropolis Mega Dreamstone** — hidden in the depths of the **Mirroh Necropolis**, guarded by **House Langerin** (Section 8 — Necropolis; the Necropolis is also Chien-Pao's Leyline gate). Its location is buried so deep that **almost no one in the main cast knows it exists** — only **Madame Roma, the Blue Moon Hermit, and Kimaris Langerin** (Section 7). The late/post-game crisis turns on **Baradus discovering this stone's location** and realizing the Necropolis is Chien-Pao's Leyline point — which is why the game's final conflict takes place there.

**Why the three are scattered `[soft canon — implication, not spelled out in-story]`:** the implication is that **Cormor Dream-Eater himself scattered the three Mega Dreamstones apart** sometime before he "went missing" at the end of his ~300-year reign (Cormor's True Status, below), precisely so no one could reunite them and re-create a calamity-scale resonance. The cults' entire multi-generational project is the slow undoing of that safeguard.

**The quest — Umbra's secret main task for the prince:**

After the Eden theft scene in Act I (Section 6 — The Eden Theft Scene), **Cadmus Umbra briefs Osrid on the clandestine objective:** **recover all 9 shards of the lost Mega-Dreamstone.** This is the **secret parallel main quest** the player undertakes alongside the public-facing Trial circuit.

**Why it must be clandestine:**
- **Hidden from the cults at all costs.** If Baradus or Goma learned the empire was assembling Mega-Dreamstone shards, every shard location would become an immediate cult target. The cults are *desperate* for an intact Dreamstone catalyst (Section 8 — Cult Cosmological Framework; the Tablets Cult's *hundreds of small Dreamstones* turned to dust in the Wo-Chien ritual, and the Vessels Cult's Mt. Mirroh Mega Dreamstone *shattered into the 9 scattered shards* during the misaligned Ting-Lu incarnation — leaving both operations non-repeatable. The Tablets Cult had also **collected essentially every small Dreamstone on the continent** for its unorthodox brute-force process, so no other cult can reproduce that method even if it wanted to). **Nine assembled shards = the cults' single greatest prize and most dangerous tool — if they could acquire even one shard, much less several, the timeline of cult-Treasure-incarnation would accelerate dramatically.**
- **Hidden from the broader Cormorian public.** The empire's official intelligence apparatus is compromised (Silas Moray as Vizier of Commerce controls much of it). A public quest for the shards would leak through cult-aligned officials to Baradus immediately.
- **Hidden even from Rhydia (during the first half of the game).** Rhydia is the public Champion and the focus of cult intelligence-gathering. Compartmentalizing the shard-hunt from her keeps her free of the *operational* knowledge that would make her a target for cult interrogation. **Umbra's broader pattern of compartmentalizing operational knowledge from his beloved pupil** (which causes the Umbra-Rhydia tension at the ~50% reveal — see Section 7 — Cadmus Umbra) extends to the shard-hunt.

**Intelligence source:**

The intelligence on the shard locations comes from **Nemo Korolev's private mercenary counter-intelligence network** (Section 7 — Nemo Korolev). Nemo's network has, over the years he has been Trial Baron, gathered fragmentary intelligence on:
- Where the original Mega-Dreamstone shattering occurred and which directions the fragments dispersed
- Which Cormorian figures have, knowingly or unknowingly, become custodians of individual shards
- Which shards have been lost to ruins, caves, or other geographical concealment
- Which shards are at risk of being discovered by the cults independently

**Nemo provides this intelligence to Umbra through their long-standing personal trust.** Umbra deputizes Osrid as the operational mover. **Nemo does not know Osrid is the prince** during the first half of the game; he knows only that *Umbra has a trusted operative* recovering the shards on the empire's behalf. The compartmentalization preserves the tight 3-person axis (Umbra → Osrid; Nemo → Umbra) without anyone other than Umbra holding full operational visibility.

**The Treasures can regenerate `[LOCKED]`:**

This is a critical worldbuilding fact. The four Treasures of Ruin are cosmic-tier embodiments of negative emotion. Their physical bodies *can be destroyed* under sufficient force (e.g., Koraidon's Breaking Swipe destroys Chi-Yu's body during the Mt. Ceram beat), but **they can regenerate under the right conditions**.

This is why **sealing — not destruction — is the only solution to the cult crisis.**
- Destruction fails: the Treasures reconstitute given time
- Sealing succeeds: the Treasures become *contained* rather than annihilated
- The Paldean king's canonical sealing of the Treasures (per SV lore) is what holds them across the two-millennium gap until DM's era
- **Osrid's sealing ritual at the climax is the originating seal** that the Paldean king inherits and reinforces ~2000 years later

The regeneration mechanic also explains why **Chi-Yu can return as the third Treasure in the final battle** despite its body being destroyed by Koraidon at the 70% mark. The cult-controlled environment of Baradus's final-battle stronghold provides the conditions for re-incarnation.

### Silas Moray's Memory-Erasure Mechanics `[LOCKED]`

The psychic mind-influence Silas uses on Pierra (and others) is mechanically constrained:

- **Short-term memory only** — erasures cover hours to days. Cannot erase years, cannot erase core identity memories, cannot erase fundamental sense of self.
- **Requires reinforcement** — erasures fade under sufficient emotional reinforcement (strong emotional events, contradictory evidence). This is why Silas must maintain Pierra's manipulation *daily*, not set-and-forget. It is sustained psychic labor.
- **Why Silas doesn't erase Osrid's memory on first encounter:** the limitation makes such a quick erasure cost-prohibitive (Osrid would re-encounter contradictions immediately, fragmenting the erasure). Only sustained presence and ongoing reinforcement enables long-term mind-control.
- **In-game implications:** this constraint prevents memory-erasure from becoming a plot-convenience tool. Silas cannot just erase the player's progress or "reset" the narrative.

### Behemoth Bash (Tinkatink-line Cormorian variant) — Custom Move `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`

A custom move designed specifically for the Tinkatink starter line, replacing the canonical Zamazenta signature move's data with a Cormorian variant that defines the Tinkatink line's signature offensive identity.

**Stats:**
- **Type:** Normal · **Category:** Physical · **Power:** 100 · **Accuracy:** 95% · **PP:** 10
- **Learner:** Tinkatuff at Lv 30 (with Tinkatink → Tinkatuff evolving at Lv 24 canonically, so the move first becomes accessible on the mid-stage); Tinkaton inherits it via standard evolutionary learnset persistence

**Custom property — Steel-effectiveness override `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

Behemoth Bash deals **super-effective damage to Steel-type Pokemon regardless of the move's resolved type after type-changing abilities such as Pixilate.** This is a *move property*, not a *type-chart override* — the effectiveness multiplier vs. Steel is hardcoded into the move's damage calculation, independent of whatever type the move resolves to.

**Pixilate interaction — the line's signature offensive identity:**

- **Base move:** Normal physical, 100 BP, super-effective vs. Steel (custom property)
- **With Pixilate active (Hidden Ability):** Normal → Fairy, ×1.2 power multiplier → 120 effective BP Fairy move
- **Against Dragon targets:** Fairy is 2× into Dragon → **240 BP effective damage vs. Dragon**
- **Against Steel targets:** Steel-effectiveness override fires regardless of Fairy retyping → 2× × 120 BP = **240 BP effective damage vs. Steel**
- **Against neutral targets:** 120 BP Fairy-typed STAB hit (with Tinkaton's 85 Atk this is a respectable but not overwhelming damage profile)

**Result:** *The only move in the entire game that hits both Dragon-types and Steel-types for super-effective damage.* Combined with the Tinkatink line's defensive bulk (Tinkaton 125/125 Def/SpD, Battle Armor crit-immunity option, HP 75) and Pixilate's STAB boost, the Pixilate + Behemoth Bash build can survive most threats and KO the two type-categories most resistant to ordinary offense. **The Tinkatink line becomes the *dragon-killer and steel-killer specialist* of the starter trio** — mechanically the perfect expression of its bloodline heritage (Section 6 — Tinkatink Line Dragon-Slayer Bloodline).

**Thematic resonance:**

The line's mechanical kit is *retroactively perfect* for its bloodline lore. Pixilate (locked v0.9.2, before the v0.9.7 dragon-slayer-clan lore was developed) is canonically *the Fairy-retyping ability designed for dragon-slaying*; Behemoth Bash (v0.9.7) extends that dragon-killer profile to *also* cover Steel. **The retroactive alignment is so clean it reads as if Pixilate was always meant for this line** — when in fact the design choice (Pixilate as Tinkatink HA) preceded the bloodline lore by several patch versions. **This is one of the project's happiest retroactive lore-mechanics resonances.**

**Engineering implementation:**

- **Zamazenta does not appear in Dreamstone Ruination** — not in any encounter table, cinematic, or cult roster. The implementation approach is to *overwrite* the canonical Behemoth Bash entry rather than create a new move. **Same MOVE_BEHEMOTH_BASH constant**, modified power/accuracy/PP/effect to match the Tinkatink-line variant, modified learnset assignment to remove from Zamazenta (irrelevant) and add to the Tinkatink line (Lv 30 on Tinkatuff/Tinkaton).
- **The Steel-effectiveness override requires custom engine support** — a new MOVE_EFFECT flag (or equivalent) that the damage calculator checks: *"if move has Steel-effectiveness-override property, multiply final damage by 2× when target is Steel-type."* This is the only engine-level new code for the move; the rest is data modification.
- **Engineering effort:** moderate. Approximately 30-40 lines of code: move data modification (~5 lines), new MOVE_EFFECT flag (~10 lines including enum + handler), damage calculator integration (~15 lines), learnset assignment (~5 lines), localization (~5 lines).
- See Section 14 — Production Discipline for full engineering tracking.

### Pact-Extended Longevity — The Three-Tier Framework `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

The mechanical/cosmological framework that explains the multiple long-lived characters in the project. **Per Section 2 — Cormorian founding myth and Section 7 — Madame Roma:**

**Tier 1 — Cormor himself:**

- **True immortality with no drawbacks** — the most extreme cosmic blessing in Pokemon history
- Hidden from his closest companions (Cormor never told Lethys or Roma; see Section 9 — Cormor's True Status below)
- Unique in Pokemon history
- Still effectively present in some sleeping/dormant form `[OPEN]` per Cormor's True Status

**Tier 2 — Cormor's inner circle (cosmic-tier longevity):**

- **Lethys / Blue Moon Hermit** — Celestica baseline (already long-lived as tribal heritage) + Keerin pact-blessing through blood-relation and proximity to Cormor. **Easily thousands of years.** Still alive in present-day game (~2000 years post-founding).
- **Madame Roma / Roma Langerin** — Not Celestica by birth, but pact-blessed via *instrumental role in the erasing ritual* (miko consecration of the four objects) AND received the *original doubly-blessed Langerin name* (Arceus + Keerin double-blessing). **Centuries to potentially thousands of years.** Still alive in present-day game.
- **Cogita** (canonical PLA character; Lethys's niece) — Celestica baseline + pact-blessing-by-blood-relation-to-Lethys (one degree removed from Cormor). Long-lived; still alive in canonical PLA era (~2500 years post-founding). See Section 12 — Cogita cross-canon connection.

**Tier 3 — Later Cormorian noble houses (standard longevity):**

- Founders of later noble houses (House Moray, House Ashland, House Surge, House Raptora, House Alban, House Jalviss): received *Arceus-blessing only* (the Keerin-component had diminished across centuries of surname-grantings)
- **Standard human lifespan with vitality blessings**
- None of them are alive in present-day Cormoria
- Their bloodlines continue but no longer carry pact-blessed longevity

**The mechanism:**

Cormor's pact with Keerin extended his lifespan substantially — but Keerin's blessing also *cascaded* outward to those who shared a deep bond with Cormor by proximity or blood:
- Direct partnership-in-pact-mission proximity (Lethys, Roma) → cosmic-tier extension
- Blood relation to Cormor through Lethys (Cogita) → cosmic-tier extension
- First Arceus-blessed surname recipient (Roma) → additional doubly-blessed extension
- Later surname recipients → progressively diminished extension (Keerin component fades over time)

**This is internally consistent mechanism design.** One foundational mechanism (the Keerin pact's cascading blessing) with *layered amplifications* (Celestica heritage, blood-relation degree, first-recipient doubled-blessing) explains the precise distribution of long-lived characters across canonical Pokemon canon.

**Implications for Kimaris and modern House Langerin:**

Kimaris Langerin (Section 7 — Kimaris) has **standard mortal lifespan** despite being a descendant of two Tier 2 ancestors (Lethys and Roma). The pact-blessing did NOT pass to subsequent generations of House Langerin — only the *original* recipients (Lethys, Roma, Cogita) carry the cosmic-tier longevity. Their descendants inherit *powers* (Marshadow generational ace, Distortion gate guardianship) but not longevity.

### Cormor's True Status — Immortality and the Avalon Sleep `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

The deepest character secret in Cormoria's cosmological history — known with certainty only to Keerin, suspected by Lethys and Roma, unknown to everyone else.

#### Cormor's actual nature `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

**Cormor is *immortal*** — true, unbridled immortality with no drawbacks. Keerin granted this to Cormor as the pact's primary effect.

**Critical detail: Cormor did NOT tell Lethys or Roma about his immortality.** Keerin told *Cormor* the full extent of the pact's effects; Cormor chose to keep this from his closest companions across the entire pact-era and the empire's two centuries of building.

**Why hide it?**
- Possibly because Cormor knew Lethys and Roma would worry about him being condemned to outlive everyone he loved
- Possibly because Cormor himself didn't yet know how he felt about it and didn't want to share an unprocessed reality
- Possibly because revealing it would have *changed* his relationships with them — they would have seen him as fundamentally different from themselves
- *This was Cormor's first secret from his closest people.* A man who had spent his pre-pact life in open partnership with his brother-friend Lethys began keeping the deepest truth about himself private — *because the cosmic weight was too much to share.*

**Lethys and Roma never learned the truth in life.** Even across centuries living together, they understood Cormor as long-lived (like themselves) rather than immortal-with-no-drawbacks. **They still don't know in the present day.** Their late-game hints about Cormor "possibly sleeping" are based on *suspicion* born of long friendship, not knowledge of his true cosmic status.

#### The three centuries of weariness and the Avalon sleep `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

**Cormor became weariest of life by the time he had ruled for three centuries.** Three hundred years of empire-building, three hundred years of seeing companions die, three hundred years of carrying the cosmic weight of having saved reality. **The most blessed man in Pokemon history became the most tired.**

**This is the project's deepest character-tragedy thread.** Immortality without drawbacks is itself the drawback — Cormor was given a gift no other human ever received, and the gift slowly *exhausted his spirit*.

**The Dreamstone time-path resolution:**

Cormor and Keerin found a Dreamstone and used it to **open a path to the distant past.** This establishes Dreamstones as time-displacement tools (Section 9 — Dreamstone, updated above). Cormor found himself a place where he could **fall into a dreamless sleep** and rest his weary soul **until Keerin believes the time is ready to awaken him.**

**Design elements:**
- **Dreamless sleep** — not unconscious, not deathlike, but *true rest*. Cormor's consciousness is quieted entirely; he does not dream.
- **Keerin is the awakener** — *not* a hero, *not* a crisis-detection mechanism, *not* Arceus's intervention. **Keerin alone decides when Cormor wakes.** This is the partnership-thesis at its most cosmologically pure — *the partner-Pokemon judges the trainer's readiness to act.*
- **Whether the time ever comes is *never known* by design** — the question is permanently open

#### The Avalon / Schrödinger framing `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

This is **a permanent open question by design**. The story does NOT resolve it.

**Inspirations:**
- **King Arthur and Avalon** — the mythological pattern of the once-and-future king resting in a sacred place until a future cosmic-need calls him back
- **Schrödinger's Cat** — the quantum-cosmological framework of an entity simultaneously being and not-being until observed

**Synthesis:**
- **Avalon-pattern:** Cormor is the sleeping founder-king awaiting cosmic need
- **Schrödinger-pattern:** Cormor is simultaneously *dead* and *alive* — neither status confirmed; both possible at once until a future observer collapses the wave function

**Why the cult crisis does NOT wake Cormor:**

The cult crisis (the Treasures of Ruin / Sword Cult Necropolis Assault / 7v9 final battle) is **resolved by Osrid**, Cormor's only living male-line direct descendant in the present day. **A crisis that would require the greatest hero of all time to re-awaken has not materialized.**

> "Since Osrid manages to resolve the catastrophe with the Treasures Of Ruin, a crisis that would require the greatest hero of all time to re-awaken has not materialized yet. It would have to be a crisis beyond all other crises to justify his awakening."

This is the *gorgeous* generational-narrative completion: **Cormor doesn't need to wake because his descendant has proven worthy of his legacy.** The partnership-thesis re-enacts itself across generations — Cormor + Keerin saved the world from Phancero through partnership; Osrid + his starter + Ting-Lu save Cormoria from the Treasures of Ruin through partnership. **The grandson does not need the grandfather to wake because the grandson has inherited the grandfather's capacity for partnership-tier accomplishment.**

#### Lethys's and Roma's late-game hints `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

**These are character-level lore-delivery beats — *implied*, not explicit.**

**Lethys (the Blue Moon Hermit)** uses specific verbal cadences when referring to Cormor:
- Present-perfect verbs implying continued existence: *"he rests"* rather than *"he died"*
- Framings that suggest interrupted-not-ended state: *"he completed his work and stepped away"* rather than *"he passed"*
- Players who pay attention will sense the implication without it being stated

**Madame Roma (Roma Langerin)** carries similar verbal patterns — *centuries* of conversation with Cormor have given her phrasings that imply continued engagement, not cessation. Her hints derive from her two-centuries co-architect partnership with Cormor rather than Lethys's deep-brother bond — *different angle, same implication*.

**Together they form a complementary pair of attentive observers** who suspect — but cannot confirm — the truth of their friend's fate. Players who hear both sets of hints reach the strongest possible inference that Cormor sleeps rather than dies.

### The Difficulty Modes and Mechanical Frameworks `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

Three player-chosen difficulty modes plus toggleable Nuzlocke and Randomizer options. This is the project's mechanical-design framework that allows different audiences to engage with the project at their preferred challenge level.

**Mode selection occurs at game start** (before character creation), and the chosen mode applies to the entire playthrough. **The player cannot change modes mid-game** — once chosen, the mode locks for that save file.

#### Classic Mode — The Casual Tier

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| **Battle style** | Switch / Set toggleable; player choice |
| **Level caps** | None — player can over-level if they grind |
| **Battle items** | Full canonical Pokemon item economy; unlimited use during battle |
| **Enemy difficulty** | Fixed difficulty curve — enemies do not scale with player |
| **Enemy IV/EV spreads** | Competitive (trainers still tuned, but not max IVs) |
| **Keerin sidequest** | **NOT available** — Classic players cannot do the Mt. Silver Future-Red quest |
| **Nuzlocke options** | NOT available |

**Design intent:** Casual player. Wants to experience the story. Wants meaningful but not punishing combat. **The "experience the game" tier.**

The exclusion of Keerin from Classic is intentional. Keerin is *not* a casual reward; she is the *cosmic-tier apex* and only players who choose meaningful difficulty earn the opportunity to face the Future-Red test.

#### Elite Mode — The Standard Hardcore Tier

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| **Battle style** | Set mode only — no switching to swap during turn opportunities |
| **Level caps** | Moderate — the Trial Baron's highest-level Pokemon always *matches* the player's current level cap |
| **Battle items** | **Only one item per battle** (or one item per round in multi-stage battles unless story specifies otherwise) |
| **Enemy IV spreads** | **Maximum IVs (31 across all stats)** for every enemy trainer Pokemon |
| **Enemy EV spreads** | **Scaling EVs** — gradually increasing as the player progresses; mid-game opponents have partial EVs, late-game opponents have full EVs |
| **Keerin sidequest** | **Available** with **Opportunist** ability (not Wonder Guard) |
| **Nuzlocke options** | **Toggleable from in-game menu** |
| **Starter Nuzlocke exemption** | **The starter cannot be permanently removed** even under Nuzlocke; KO'd starter does not "die" |

**Design intent:** Players who want a meaningful challenge but don't need maximum-difficulty cosmic-tier ceiling. **The "play seriously" tier.** Most engaged players who want a real challenge will choose Elite.

**The Keerin Opportunist mechanic:** Opportunist (canonical Pokemon ability from Scarlet/Violet) copies any stat boost the opponent gains. This is *thematically resonant* — Keerin observes and adapts. It's also *significantly less broken than Wonder Guard* — strong competitive ability but not near-invincibility.

#### Paragon Mode — The Hardcore Competitive Tier

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| **Battle style** | Set mode only |
| **Level caps** | **Harsh** — the two strongest Pokemon of every Trial Baron, Vizier, and major boss fight is always **5 levels higher** than the player's level cap |
| **Battle items** | One item per battle (same as Elite) |
| **Enemy IVs** | Maximum (31 across all stats) |
| **Enemy EVs** | **Optimized max** (252 in primary stat / 252 in secondary stat / 4 in tertiary stat — the canonical EV-optimization pattern) for every Pokemon in addition to maxed IVs |
| **Enemy movesets** | **Perfectly optimized** — no filler moves; every slot is competitive |
| **Enemy AI** | **Especially ruthless** — possibly using maximum-difficulty AI patterns (predictive switching, optimal move selection, item-use timing) |
| **Player starter IVs/EVs** | **Perfect IVs and EV spread from the very beginning** — no grinding required |
| **Keerin sidequest** | **Available** with full **Wonder Guard** + perfect IVs + perfect EV spread |
| **Nuzlocke options** | Available (same toggleable system as Elite); starter exempt |

**Design intent:** Hardcore competitive players. Extreme-difficulty enthusiasts. **The "test yourself against the project's maximum" tier.** Recommended only for players who specifically seek brutal challenge.

**The Wonder Guard Keerin** is the *full* cosmic-tier reward — only obtainable through completing the Mt. Silver Future-Red test in Paragon mode. **Paragon players who beat Future-Red receive the most powerful Pokemon entity in any project.**

#### The Nuzlocke Toggle System

**Nuzlocke is a *toggleable* option** rather than a separate mode. Players in Elite or Paragon can opt into Nuzlocke rules and toggle them off later if they want a less brutal experience for any segment.

**Standard Nuzlocke rules supported (`[OPEN]` confirm specific rules):**
- KO'd Pokemon are released / permanently removed from the player's team (the "death" rule)
- Only the first encounter in each route can be caught (the "first encounter" rule)
- Optional: nickname-everything rule (purely cosmetic)
- Optional: species clause (cannot catch a Pokemon of a species already in your party)

**The starter exemption:**

**The starter is *exempt* from Nuzlocke "death."** Even with Nuzlocke active, a KO'd starter does not get permanently removed from the team. The starter's cosmic narrative significance is preserved under all play modes. **The starter cannot be Nuzlocke-permanently-deleted.**

This integrates with the unboxable-starter mechanic (Section 6) — the starter is structurally protected from both PC deposit and Nuzlocke removal.

#### The Randomizer System

**Randomizer option available** to players who want randomized Pokemon. Many configurable settings:
- `[OPEN]` Specific randomizer options — likely include: randomize wild encounters, randomize trainer teams, randomize abilities, randomize movesets, randomize hold items
- `[OPEN]` Compatibility with Nuzlocke options + difficulty modes (likely all are independent toggles)

**Starter exclusion:**

**The starter and its evolutions are NOT randomized regardless of randomizer settings.** Frigibax → Baxcalibur, Teddiursa (Blue Moon) → Bloodmoon Ursaluna (Blue Moon), and Tinkatink → Tinkaton remain the only three possible starter selections under any randomization configuration. The starter's cosmic narrative significance is preserved under all play modes.
