## 6. The Starter Trio and Umbra's Gift Scene

### The gift scene `[LOCKED]`

After the prince returns home and reconnects with Rhydia, **Vizier Umbra** presents him with a choice between three Pokémon — hand-selected over years of search. Umbra's dialogue (paraphrased; refine later):

> *"These Pokémon are the rarest of the rare and uniquely colored in a way that has never been seen before in these species. You are our one and only Prince, and it is fitting that you begin your journey with a Pokémon partner that befits your role in the world. You will carry a heavy weight, and you need a unique power to rise above it. These three Pokémon I have painstakingly sought out possess that power."*

The player chooses one. The other two are not encountered in the wild — they are *uniquely-bred individuals* in Umbra's care.

### The Eden theft scene — Act I inciting incident `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`

Immediately after the gift scene, **Eden breaks into the basement of Umbra's estate in the starter town** (Silver/Crystal homage explicitly intended). She steals **the starter that is mechanically strongest against Osrid's pick**, leaving the third with Umbra.

**The type-counter steal logic `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

| Osrid picks | Eden steals | Stays with Umbra | Counter logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frigibax (Dragon/Ice) | **Tinkatink** (Fairy/Steel) | Teddiursa | Fairy is 2× into Dragon; Steel resists Ice. Eden's stolen Tinkatink is *the dragon-slayer-bloodline pick weaponized against the dragon-line Osrid chose.* |
| Teddiursa (Normal → Normal/Ground) | **Frigibax** (Dragon/Ice) | Tinkatink | Ice STAB hits Ground super-effectively (Bloodmoon Ursaluna); Dragon-type raw offense is a special-attack threat against the Normal/Ground special-attacker Osrid raised. |
| Tinkatink (Fairy/Steel) | **Teddiursa** (Normal → Normal/Ground) | Frigibax | Ground hits Steel super-effectively (the late-game Bloodmoon Ursaluna's Ground STAB demolishes Tinkaton's Steel half). |

**The narrative function:**
- **Eden begins her bond-through-theft arc.** This is the inciting moment of *her* deuteragonist journey (Section 7 — Eden, expanded v0.9.7). She does not yet love the Pokemon she just stole. She will. Over the course of the game, **the stolen starter becomes the partner she chose by accident and kept by love.**
- **Eden keeps the stolen starter through the entire game — including post-game and her eventual marriage to Veddev in Paldea.** *Their post-game marriage is between two people who each still carry their original starter.* The mirror is the resolution: rivalry → true love, theft → bond, the partnership-thesis applied to *their own relationship* as well as to Pokemon.
- **The unstolen-third-starter stays with Umbra.** This Pokemon plays no role until the Act V Vizier gauntlet, where **Umbra deploys it as part of his team in the first Vizier fight** (see Section 10 — Act V; Section 7 — Cadmus Umbra). The third starter Osrid did not choose becomes one of the Pokemon Osrid must defeat to become Champion. **All three starters are present in the climactic political-progression arc:** the player's pick is in the player's party, Eden's pick is on Eden's team, Umbra's pick is on Umbra's team. **No starter is ever wasted; all three are part of the story's spine.**

**Eden's targeting was not random:**
- Eden has type-matchup knowledge appropriate to a *competent trainer*, not an amateur thief. She picked the *mechanically optimal* counter-starter, not a random target.
- This implies *someone* — likely Goma personally, or possibly a Beads Cult bishop — *directed* her to the basement with specific intelligence about Osrid's starter choice. **The theft is a targeted operation, not a freelance crime.** The Beads Cult's intelligence apparatus is documented in Section 8 (Goma's pre-Mt-Ceram operational capability).
- **Eden does not yet know she's being used.** She believes the theft is her own clever crime; she will eventually learn that her cult handlers leveraged her for this op. Her defection trajectory (Section 7 — Eden) accelerates partly because *she realizes she was a tool*.

**Umbra's response and the Mega-Dreamstone Shard briefing:**
- After Eden escapes, **Umbra briefs Osrid on a parallel main quest** (Section 9 — The Mega-Dreamstone Shard Quest): clandestinely recover the **9 scattered shards** of Cormoria's lost Mega-Dreamstone, intelligence on which comes from **Nemo Korolev's counter-intelligence network**. This briefing is the moment Osrid learns about the secret cosmological objective running parallel to the public Trial circuit.
- **The shard-quest briefing is conditional on the theft having just happened.** Umbra waits for the theft because (a) it confirms cult intelligence has noticed Osrid's existence, raising the stakes; (b) it gives Umbra moral cover to deputize the prince for clandestine operations the empire's official apparatus cannot be trusted with.

**The starter trio's offensive/defensive trinity `[LOCKED]`:**

Each starter occupies a distinct mechanical role:

- **Frigibax → Baxcalibur** — *I want to hit hard physically and have priority + Mega options*
- **Teddiursa (Blue Moon) → Bloodmoon Ursaluna (Blue Moon)** — *I want to nuke things with special attacks and have Ground coverage with snowball potential*
- **Tinkatink → Tinkaton** — *I want to outlast everything and counter-punch with defensive reliability + Pixilate offense*

**No two are interchangeable. No pick is objectively weaker.** Each path has a clear Trial-6 Master Tutor evolution gate, a memorable ability suite creating multiple builds, a distinct combat identity, and a post-Champion Ancient Cormoria side quest that unlocks the line's coronation-tier bonus item. **This is the design level where players love their starter choice for personal mechanical reasons, not just narrative ones.**

### Unified starter-trio BST framework `[LOCKED v0.9.8]`

All three starter lines share an identical BST budget at each stage:

| Stage | BST | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 375 | All three Stage 1s have the same total stat budget |
| 2 | 480 | All three Stage 2s have the same total stat budget |
| 3 | 600 | All three Stage 3s have the same total stat budget |

The intent is that **no starter is BST-disadvantaged at any progression point.** Each line distributes those 375/480/600 points differently to express its distinct role identity (see "Three-line role identity framework" below).

Each Stage 3 has one signature stat at 145 OR paired stats at 125/125:

| Line | Stage 3 | Signature stat(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Frigibax | Baxcalibur | Atk 145 |
| Teddiursa Blue Moon | Bloodmoon Ursaluna | SpA 145 |
| Tinkatink | Tinkaton | Def 125 paired with SpD 125 |

This signature-stat anchoring makes each Stage 3 mechanically distinctive — at apex, each starter has a clear "best stat" identity that other starters can't match.

### Three-line role identity framework `[LOCKED v0.9.8]`

Each starter line dominates 2 stat slots, is mid-pack in 2, and trails in 2. The role differentiation is visible at every progression stage:

| Role attribute | Frigibax (high) | Teddiursa Blue Moon (high) | Tinkatink (high) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attack | **80 / 110 / 145** | 50 / 65 / 75 | 70 / 85 / 110 |
| Sp. Attack | dump (30 / 40 / 55) | **80 / 110 / 145** | dump (30 / 40 / 50) |
| Speed | **80 / 105 / 120** | 60 / 75 / 90 | 50 / 60 / 75 |
| Defense | 55 / 70 / 85 | 60 / 75 / 95 | **85 / 110 / 125** |
| Sp. Defense | 65 / 70 / 95 | 70 / 80 / 100 | **75 / 95 / 125** |
| HP | 65 / 85 / 100 | 55 / 75 / 95 | 65 / 90 / 115 |

**The three identities expressed as one-liners:**

- **Frigibax line:** physical-attacker + speed identity; moderate HP; low Def/SpD; dump SpA. Cormorian dragon empire's chosen successor.
- **Teddiursa Blue Moon line:** special-attacker apex; moderate Speed and bulk; lowest HP of trio. The lunar-mystic mind-and-voice line. The trio's coverage king (Ground / Fairy / Psychic / Fire / Fighting / Ghost access).
- **Tinkatink line:** defensive colossus; paired Def + SpD identity; highest HP at apex; lowest Speed of trio; moderate Atk; dump SpA. Cormorian dragon-slayer-clan inheritor.

**Both Frigibax and Tinkatink dump SpA `[LOCKED v0.9.8]`** because their kits are **100% physical-attacker** — every damage-dealing move on either line is a physical attack. SpA points on these lines would be wasted stats. Only the Blue Moon Teddiursa line uses SpA, and its kit is correspondingly all-special.

### Mechanical implementation `[LOCKED]`

- **All three are true shinies with new custom palettes** (not the canonical shiny colors)
- Requires designing 9 new sprite palettes (3 evolution lines × 3 stages each), plus their overworld variants
- Shiny status is flagged genuinely in the data — they are mechanically shinies, not cosmetic-only
- **Each starter line has its OWN unique shiny palette identity** — the three starters do NOT share a visual scheme. Each gets a one-of-a-kind look so that a player's choice of starter results in a genuinely distinctive partner.
- Within an evolution line (e.g., Frigibax → Arctibax → Baxcalibur), the palette identity stays consistent so the line reads as a cohesive family.
- **Unified starter-trio BST framework `[LOCKED v0.9.8]`** — all three starter lines share the same BST budget at each stage: **375 / 480 / 600** for Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3. This SUPERSEDES the prior v0.9.7 Tinkaton stat lock (BST 560, 75/85/125/75/125/75) and the v0.9.5 Bloodmoon Ursaluna lock (BST 560, 113/75/85/145/90/52). Each Stage 3 has one signature stat at 145 (Baxcalibur Atk 145, Bloodmoon Ursaluna SpA 145) OR paired 125s (Tinkaton Def/SpD 125/125). See "Base stats" subsection below for full per-stage tables across all three lines.

### The Tinkatink Line — Cormorian Dragon-Slayer Bloodline `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`

The Tinkatink line in Dreamstone Ruination is *not* a wild-caught Tinkatink. It is a **uniquely-bloodlined individual** from an ancient dragon-slayer clan whose existence is the in-world explanation for Cormoria's chronic dragon scarcity.

**The Tinkaton Matriarch — the sleeping ancient `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

Deep in the **far desert and surrounding mountains** of Cormoria lies an ancient cave system. Within it sleeps an **ancient Tinkaton Matriarch** — a Pokemon so old that her active leadership of her clan has long since passed into deep slumber. She is still alive; she simply *rests*, having earned her sleep after centuries of dragon-hunting.

**Her clan still lives in the desert and mountains around her cave.** Her uniquely-colored descendants — the dragon-slayer bloodline — defend the territory she carved out across centuries. **Their primary prey are Garchomp, Flygon, and the occasional Sandaconda** — dragons and dragon-egg-group Pokemon that wander the desert. The clan has been at this work *for so long* that they have effectively driven Cormoria's wild dragon population to near-extinction.

**This is the in-world explanation for several locked details across the bible:**
- **Why dragons are vanishingly rare in Cormoria** (Section 7 — Glaive). The empire credits Glaive as the first true dragon-master because *most Cormorians who might have pursued Dragon mastery had no dragons to find*.
- **Why the Solomonar title has been given only twice in 2000 years** (Section 2 — Solomonar title). The ecological suppression of dragons is centuries-old; opportunities for mastery are correspondingly rare.
- **Why the Frigibax line is so striking as a starter pick.** Umbra is presenting Osrid with a *dragon* in a region where wild dragons have been hunted to near-vanishing for centuries. *That* is why Frigibax was hand-selected as one of three starters — it represents the dragon-lineage that the empire has nearly lost.
- **Why Glaive is wary of the Tinkaton clan** (Section 7 — Glaive, updated v0.9.7). His Salamence is the *exact* kind of Pokemon the clan considers prey; his Sandaconda is *literally* listed as one of their primary kills. His unease is concrete, not abstract.

**The Matriarch as a parallel to Cormor `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

The Tinkaton Matriarch occupies a *Pokemon-cosmological-tier longevity* slot that mirrors but does not duplicate Cormor's Avalon sleep (Section 9 — Cormor's True Status):
- **Cormor sleeps** in a distant-past Dreamstone-displaced location, awakening conditional on Keerin's judgment of cosmic need
- **The Matriarch sleeps** in an ancient cave in the deep desert, awakening conditional on a sufficient threat to her clan's territory

**Both are ancient beings retreated into deep slumber. Both are still alive. Both can wake.** The Matriarch is *not* pact-blessed (she falls outside the Cormor-Lethys-Roma-Cogita Pact-Extended Longevity framework of Section 9); her longevity is *Pokemon-cosmological-tier ancient* — a different mechanism entirely.

**The mid-game Tinkatink clan side quest serves as the project's small-scale rehearsal of the larger Cormor-might-wake possibility.** When the player intrudes deep enough into the cave during the mid-game quest, the Matriarch *does* wake — proving that ancient sleepers can be roused by sufficient cause. *This is the only such awakening the player witnesses in the game.* Cormor's potential awakening remains permanently open per design (Section 9). The Matriarch's awakening is the *limited* version of what Cormor's would be — a Pokemon-tier echo of the cosmic-tier mystery.

**The Tinkatink starter's specific origin `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

Umbra visited the desert region during his years of searching for the three perfect starter partners. While in the clan's territory, **he found an egg** of the unique bloodline coloration. **The strong implication is that he took the egg without the clan's permission** — "stolen under their noses." He returned to his workshop with it and hatched it. The hatchling Tinkatink became the third of his three hand-selected starters.

**The starter Tinkatink does not know its heritage.** It was hatched in Umbra's workshop, raised by Umbra and his staff, and has no memory of the desert or the clan. To the Tinkatink, Umbra is its first family; Osrid (when chosen) becomes its second.

**Umbra knows the heritage.** And **Umbra briefly tells Osrid the truth** during a return visit to the workshop after the starter selection — *conditional on Osrid having chosen Tinkatink*. The scene is brief, almost offhand: Umbra acknowledges that the Tinkatink came from the desert clan, that he took the egg, and that the clan is one of the oldest Pokemon families in Cormoria. He does *not* dwell on the moral weight of the theft; **Umbra is, by his own private moral calculus, willing to commit lesser wrongs in service of preserving the empire's royal succession** (this is consistent with his bible-locked role of constructing Osrid's Nomad-Alban false identity, withholding Osrid's return from Rhydia for months, etc.). The egg-theft is, for Umbra, one of many quietly-carried compromises.

**Glaive's response to a Tinkatink-pick player `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

Glaive's pre-battle and post-battle dialogue varies slightly depending on whether Osrid chose Tinkatink:
- **If Osrid chose Frigibax:** Glaive's locked v0.9.6 dialogue applies — extra respectful acknowledgment of the Frigibax as a fellow dragon (psychic-empathic recognition).

- **If Osrid chose Teddiursa:** Glaive's dialogue is the neutral baseline — no Frigibax-recognition, no Tinkatink-wariness.

**The mid-game Tinkatink clan side quest `[LOCKED v0.9.7 — structural lock; details TBD]`:**

- **Bloodmoon-Ursaring-bearing player:** Neutral encounter (the clan has no specific ecological grudge against bears).

**Combat structure:** The player fights multiple **Tinkatinks** of the clan, then a few **Tinkatuffs**, then eventually **the Matriarch awakens** and the player faces her. **The Matriarch is guarding a Dreamstone fragment** — one of the 9 shards of the lost Mega-Dreamstone (Section 9 — Mega-Dreamstone Shard Quest). Defeating her is the *only* way to recover this shard.

**Why this design is structurally significant:**

1. **Real player choice with real consequences.** Most starter ultimate-power-up mechanics are mandatory (Mega Stones, Z-Crystals, Tera-Crystals in canonical games are gated by linear progression). In Ruination, the player can choose to *skip* the bonus. This is unusual and creates meaningful tension.

2. **Mirrors the project's broader thesis about partnership.** The player can *prioritize their bond with their starter* (detour, get the bonus, deepen the personal relationship) *or prioritize the empire's defense* (skip the detour, hurry to the climax). Both choices are valid. Both reflect different values. This is the project's thesis at the player-choice level — *partnership with your starter has its own value, distinct from the political-military mission.*

3. **Significantly increases late-game replayability.** Three starter choices × detour-or-skip × different quest content per starter = **six distinct late-game Act VI experiences**. Players who replay with different starters see different content in a familiar location.

4. **The "final visit to Ancient Cormoria" framing is poetically resonant either way.** If the player takes the detour, *this is their final visit and they fully engage with it*. If they skip, *Ancient Cormoria remains forever a place they didn't fully explore* in this playthrough. Either outcome carries meaning.

5. **Bonus items are *narrative-personal*, not *political-institutional*.** Unlike a coronation gift from the state, these items are *earned* by the player going back into Ancient Cormoria one last time with their starter. The bond between trainer and Pokemon unlocks the apex — not the state. This aligns with the project's thesis (Pokemon-as-partners, not state-instruments).

**Ancient Peat Block quest (Teddiursa Blue Moon-pickers) — "The Shadow Self" `[LOCKED v0.9.3]`:**

The narrative and combat structure of this quest:

- **Companion: The Blue Moon Hermit** — the same character who served as the Trial 6 Master Tutor teaching Blood Moon (Ground) to enable the Bloodmoon Ursaluna evolution. **Their identity is now consolidated as both the Trial 6 tutor and the post-Champion quest companion** (Section 7 — Blue Moon Hermit entry).

The swamp itself is a *lunar-resonant location* — peat in a fog-shrouded ancient environment is a natural accumulator of dream-energy and emotional residue (consistent with the Dreamstone framework, Section 9, and Leyline Resonance, Section 2).
- **The encounter:** Before the player can claim the Peat Block, **a stranger approaches out of the fog — a shadow version of the player themselves.** The shadow has the player's appearance (a darker silhouette or palette-swap of the player's sprite) and *the player's own Pokemon team*.
- **Combat: 6v6 mirror match against the shadow-self**
  - The shadow opponent's team is *identical* to the player's current party — same species, same moves, same items, same stat values
  - This is *purely a battle of execution and outplaying*. No team-comp tricks can save the player; only better play wins
  - **The player's mastery of their own team is the test** — *do you know how to play your team better than someone else with the same team can?*
- **Win condition:** Defeat the shadow-self. The fog clears. The Ancient Peat Block is yours to claim.
- **Thematic resonance:** The Blue Moon Teddiursa line's lunar-mystic identity is *especially* well-suited to this encounter. Bloodmoon Ursaluna is associated with self-reflection, the unconscious, and the dream-state. **Facing one's own shadow self is *the Bloodmoon experience* taken to its logical narrative conclusion.** Additionally, this quest *obliquely mirrors Osrid's own renunciation arc* — Osrid himself has a "shadow self" (Rainhawk, Nomad Alban, Veddev), and the player encountering and defeating their own shadow before claiming the Bloodmoon Ursaluna's Lunacy transformation is *thematically resonant* with Osrid's identity reconciliation.

**Divine Cuirass quest (Tinkatink-pickers) — "The Sleeping Terror" `[LOCKED v0.9.3]`:**

The most mechanically intensive of the three quests — a multi-floor temple puzzle culminating in a five-stage combat gauntlet.

- **Companion:** *None* — this is the *only solo quest* among the three. The Tinkatink-picker undertakes it alone, fitting Tinkaton's defensive solo-survival identity.
- **Setting:** The **ancient temple where Koraidon sleeps** — *the same temple* the player visited during the Mt. Ceram Act III beat where Koraidon awakened, destroyed Chi-Yu's body with Breaking Swipe, and returned to sleep. **The Tinkatink-picker is the *only* path that returns to this location** in the post-Champion content.
- **Structure: Multi-floor puzzle-oriented dungeon** — *longer and more puzzle-oriented* than the other two quest structures. The temple has multiple floors; each presents puzzles the player must solve to descend further. **The puzzles reward methodical, strategic thinking** — exactly the cognitive style that Tinkatink-pickers signaled by their starter choice.
- **The Divine Cuirass is hidden** in a room *behind* the sleeping Koraidon's chamber. To reach it, the player must defeat Koraidon's guardian first.
- **The guardian: Hoopa Unbound** — the Mythical Pokemon canonically associated with summoning beings from other places and times. Hoopa is *itself* the dimensional-pull mechanism in Pokemon lore. In this quest, **Hoopa Unbound is the active guardian** (alert and combative), while **Koraidon is the passive guardian** (sleeping until awakened as the final stage).
- **Combat gauntlet: 5 stages, all level 100, played as a continuous sequence against a single 6-Pokemon player team:**

  1. **Blacephalon** (Ultra Beast — Fire/Ghost). Mind Blown self-destruction kit, Shadow Ball, Flamethrower, often Choice-Specs-equipped for one-turn-kill potential. *Dimensional outsider from Ultra Space.*
  2. **Galarian Moltres** (Galar regional variant — Dark/Flying). Berserk ability, Nasty Plot setup option, Fiery Wrath signature move, Hurricane coverage. *Pokemon from a far-future region (Galar) summoned across time and space.*
  3. **Iron Moth** (Future Paradox — Fire/Poison). Quark Drive ability + Booster Energy item makes it a fearsome Speed-boosted Special Attacker; Fiery Dance for self-buff, Sludge Wave, Discharge coverage. *Pokemon from a future timeline.*
  4. **Hoopa Unbound** (the guardian itself — Psychic/Dark). Magician ability, Hyperspace Fury signature move (ignores Protect, hits hard physically), incredible mixed offensive output. Possibly Choice Scarf or Life Orb-equipped. *The dimensional-pull mechanism turning its summoning power on the player.*
  5. **Koraidon** (Past Paradox — Fighting/Dragon, the sleeping terror awakened for the final fight). Orichalcum Pulse ability (sets sun + Atk boost on switch-in), Collision Course (Fighting STAB at devastating BP), Flare Blitz, Outrage. One of the strongest Pokemon in canonical Pokemon, here at level 100. *The original sleeping guardian, awakened only after Hoopa is defeated.*

- **The summoning theme:** Each opponent represents a different *temporal-cosmic* category — Ultra Space (Blacephalon), far-future canonical region (Galarian Moltres), Future Paradox (Iron Moth), dimensional Mythical (Hoopa), and Past Paradox (Koraidon). **Hoopa Unbound's gauntlet spans every cosmological axis the project has established** (Section 2 — Leyline Resonance). The fight is *cosmologically consistent* with the bible's worldbuilding.
- **Combat tier:** This is *the toughest combat encounter the project has designed outside of the 7v9 final battle*. Five stages of level-100 elite Pokemon in sequence with no team-swap recovery between stages. The Tinkatink-picker's defensive identity (Battle Armor crit-prevention, high HP, Divine Cuirass durability) is well-suited to outlasting this sequence — **the quest *tests* the defensive playstyle the line excels at**.
- **Win condition:** Defeat all 5 stages. After the final Koraidon defeat, **Koraidon returns to sleep, Hoopa Unbound disappears** (canonical for the unbinding mythical — its temporary guardianship ends), **and the Divine Cuirass is claimed from a pedestal in the room behind the sleeping Koraidon.**
- **The poetic narrative beat:** Once the gauntlet is complete, the player walks past the sleeping Koraidon to claim the Cuirass. **The sleeping terror has *let the player pass* — the test was passed.** This quiet image gives the quest emotional resonance beyond the combat.
- **Koraidon's narrative arc completes here.** This is the only quest where Koraidon is *engaged* in proper combat (during Act III, the player only witnessed Koraidon awaken and destroy Chi-Yu's body). The Tinkatink-picker's Cormorian experience uniquely includes a *closing of the Koraidon arc.*

Puzzle implementations across floors. 5-stage continuous gauntlet battle scripting (no team-swap recovery between stages; single-team-throughout structure). Custom encounter data for Blacephalon, Galarian Moltres, Iron Moth, Hoopa Unbound, Koraidon (level 100 each, competitive kits).

### The Unboxable Starter — A Foundational Mechanic `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`

**The starter Pokemon cannot be deposited into the PC system.** It must remain in the player's party throughout the entire game.

**The mechanism:**
- The standard PC system exists, framed as **the Pokemon Caretaker** at Taverns (Section 2 — Tavern Pokemon Centers, which are the project's PC-equivalent infrastructure). The Caretaker provides the canonical-feeling PC interface for depositing, withdrawing, and organizing Pokemon.
- **When the player attempts to deposit their starter, the starter refuses to enter the PC system.** A dialogue beat and/or animation conveys this — the Caretaker comments on the situation, the starter's overworld sprite reacts (possibly clinging to the player, possibly returning to its Pokeball but refusing release), the deposit action *fails*. **The starter stays in the player's party.**

**Why the design works:**

The mechanical constraint *is* the narrative beat. **The starter's cosmic significance is reinforced *every time* the player tries to box it and gets refused.** The starter is *a character*, not an interchangeable team member. Players who experiment with team composition discover their starter's special status by direct interaction with the game systems.

**Non-starter party freedom:** The player retains complete freedom over the other five party slots. They can rotate any non-starter Pokemon between the party and the PC as much as they want. **Only the starter is bound.**

**The epilogue payoff:**

The unboxable-starter mechanic enables the epilogue narrative beat (Section 5 — Phase 3 epilogue; Section 7 — Vandras entry). When Osrid is found amnesia-ridden after the climactic sealing cataclysm, his *only* Pokemon with him is his starter — and this is *guaranteed* by the unboxable mechanic. The starter being present is what enables Vandras to recognize Osrid (through the starter) and the Veddev-naming ceremony that closes the prince's arc. **The mechanic and the story are inseparable** — neither could work without the other.

**Nuzlocke interaction:** Even in Elite or Paragon difficulty modes with Nuzlocke options enabled, **the starter is *exempt* from Nuzlocke "death."** A KO'd starter does not get permanently removed from the team. The starter's cosmic narrative significance is preserved under all play modes. See Section 9 — The Difficulty Modes for the full mode framework.

**Randomizer interaction:** Even with randomizer settings active, **the starter line is NOT randomized.** Frigibax → Baxcalibur, Teddiursa (Blue Moon) → Bloodmoon Ursaluna (Blue Moon), and Tinkatink → Tinkaton remain the only three possible starter selections under any randomization configuration. See Section 9 — Randomizer System for details.

**Implementation:** PC interface modification to check the deposit-Pokemon flag against the starter-species flag; conditional refusal logic; reaction dialogue from the Tavern Pokemon Caretaker NPC; possibly a brief animation. See Section 14 — Engineering Tasks for production specifics.
