### Glaive — Dragon Tamer, future Vizier of War, future Solomonar, future founder of Blackthorn City `[LOCKED — updated v0.9.7]`

**Critical background context `[UPDATED v0.9.7]`:** Dragons in our era are generally regarded in Cormoria as **the most wild and hostile Pokémon type to humans** — *and* they are *ecologically rare* in Cormoria because of centuries of suppression by the Tinkaton clan in the deep desert (Section 6 — Tinkatink Line Dragon-Slayer Bloodline; Section 2 — Solomonar title). The combined effect: very few Cormorians ever encounter wild dragons, and those who do struggle to master them. Some trainers across Cormorian history have managed to keep a single Dragon-type on their team here and there, but **no family has ever specialized in or mastered Dragon-types in the modern empire era**. **Glaive is the first trainer in 1700+ years of Cormorian history to be officially recognized as a true dragon-master — the second Solomonar in the empire's entire 2000-year span.** Cormoria publicly acknowledges that a *first* Solomonar existed in Cormor's third century (with a shiny Haxorus ace, identity forgotten to history), but Glaive is the first dragon-master since that ancient era.

He is described in-world as having something like "the spirit of a dragon himself or the aura of one." The truth, theorized by Vizier Umbra later in the game: **Glaive has an unrecognized psychic gift** — a *dragon-empathic* ability that lets him feel the emotions of dragons and dragon-egg-group Pokémon, and psychically communicate his own emotions to them. **He does not know this about himself.** No one else knows either, except Umbra (who theorizes but does not directly tell Glaive). It lets him bond with dragons naturally in a way most humans cannot.

This makes Glaive mythically singular. **He is the founder of a tradition that hasn't started yet** — the first Cormorian dragon-master in 1700+ years, the second Solomonar in the empire's entire history (Section 2 — Solomonar title). By the time of Dreamstone Mysteries ~2000 years later, dragons are tamed normally enough that there are Dragon-type institutions, gym leaders, etc. The dragon-mastery lineage *begins* with Glaive. His personal anomaly becomes humanity's eventual normal. **And the deeper-still truth (per v0.9.7):** the dragons Glaive masters are themselves the *survivors* of centuries of Tinkaton-clan suppression. He is not just the first modern Cormorian to master a difficult type; he is the first modern Cormorian to master a type whose rarity was *actively maintained by a hostile Pokemon bloodline* over the entirety of the empire's history.

Glaive does not hate the Tinkaton clan, but he is *wary* of them. His Sandaconda (Dragon egg group, Ground) is *literally listed* among the clan's primary prey (Section 6 — Tinkatink Line). His Salamence Blackthorn is the *exact* kind of apex-dragon the clan considers their hereditary enemy. His unease is *concrete and ecological*, not abstract:

**The arc, in full:**

1. The prince's childhood friend, from before the war
2. When Osrid is sent overseas as a teenager, Glaive stays in Cormoria
3. The cults spread false news of Osrid's death
4. Glaive, devastated and enraged, captures a **rare mutated shiny Bagon** — black and crimson coloring, very imposing. He names it **Blackthorn**.
5. **Wandering the wilderness with Blackthorn `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`:** During his Bagon-training years, Glaive *wanders the Cormorian wilderness alone with Blackthorn*, intending to develop dragon-mastery through trial and error since no Cormorian tradition exists to teach him. During this period he is **caught in a brutal sandstorm** that threatens his life and Blackthorn's.
6. **Glaive is saved from the sandstorm by the Blue Moon Hermit (Section 7 — Blue Moon Hermit).** The Hermit provides shelter and survival, then keeps Glaive and Blackthorn for an extended period of recovery and teaching.
7. **The Hermit gives Glaive partnership-training.** This is *the critical event* of Glaive's pre-prince-reveal development. The Hermit recognizes Glaive's psychic-empathic gift (Section 7 — Glaive's hidden psychic gift) and teaches him how to *deepen his understanding* with Blackthorn beyond the vengeance-fueled tool-mastery he had been pursuing. **The Hermit's partnership-training is what enables Blackthorn to evolve from Bagon to Shelgon.**
8. Glaive is still ultimately driven by vengeance after leaving the Hermit's shelter — *his heart has not yet fully opened*. The Bagon → Shelgon evolution is the *first step* toward the deeper bond, not its completion. **Shelgon refuses to evolve further into Salamence** while Glaive remains pulled by vengeance.
9. By the time the prince returns to Cormoria, Glaive is regarded as Cormoria's only up-and-coming Dragon Master — a singular figure in Cormorian history, with no precedent (most Cormorians do not know about his time with the Hermit; he doesn't discuss it openly).
10. When Glaive first meets the masked Rainhawk, he does not recognize his lost friend.
11. **Mid-game major event:** the prince reveals his identity to Glaive before Glaive makes a major mistake (`[OPEN]` what the mistake is)
12. The revelation transforms Glaive — he begins to **truly open his heart** to his Shelgon. This completes the partnership-thesis arc the Hermit had begun years earlier.
13. Off-screen between scenes, **Blackthorn evolves from Shelgon into a uniquely-shiny black-and-crimson Salamence**
14. Glaive ascends to **Vizier of War** — one of the four Viziers, fought by the player as a late-game gate before Rhydia
15. After the player defeats him, Glaive's heart is turned toward peace
16. He departs Cormoria, having heard of an ancient dragon-tribe in Johto living in harmony with nature
17. He becomes the first true Master and leader of that fledgling dragon tribe and founds a city in Johto
18. **The city is named after his Salamence — Blackthorn City**
19. Lance's family eventually descends from the Dragon's Den order Glaive establishes

### Cadmus Umbra, the Pokémon Philosopher — Vizier of Education `[LOCKED]`

**Umbra is significantly more politically important than his Pokémon Philosopher title suggests. He is the architect of Osrid's protective exile, the keeper of the prince's identity secret across the entire decade between Eldred's assassination and the start of the game, *and* he has been Rhydia's personal tutor from infancy. He is the secret architect of the entire royal succession recovery plan.**

**Full name:** Cadmus Umbra. The cognomen "Cadmus" carries Greek-mythological resonance — Cadmus founded Thebes, brought the alphabet to Greece, and sowed the dragon's teeth from which warriors sprang. The educational-civilizational role tracks with his Vizier-of-Education portfolio. The dragon's-teeth motif resonates with Glaive's dragon-mastery arc — *Umbra is in some sense the seed-planter of the dragon tradition* (he gave Osrid his starter; Osrid's friend Glaive becomes Cormoria's first dragon-master). And *Cadmus is the man who taught warriors to fight by giving them the means* — which is exactly what Umbra did for Rhydia from babyhood.

**Public role:**
- Vizier of Education throughout the Cormorian Empire
- Empire-era equivalent of Professor Oak/Elm
- Ancestor of Professor Tenebris (Dreamstone Mysteries)
- Latin-shadow naming tradition (Umbra = "shadow"; Tenebris = "darkness") — deliberate family tradition
- Title "Pokémon Philosopher" rather than "Professor" reflects the pre-modern era
- A counterweight to Cormorian warrior-Pokémon culture — pushes back against weaponization-thinking even within the Empire's military traditions

**Rhydia's tutor from infancy `[LOCKED]`:**

This may be Umbra's most consequential single role. **He has tutored Rhydia personally since she was a baby.** Politics, battling, governance, philosophy, history, statesmanship — every aspect of his strategic capability has been poured into her over 20 years.

- He is essentially a *third parent* in Rhydia's life alongside Pierra (and Eldred, until his assassination)
- **Rhydia is in some sense Umbra's life work.** She is what he made when he had two decades to make her.
- His careful strategic mind is *embedded* in her thinking — she analyzes problems the way he taught her to, considers contingencies the way he taught her to, delegates the way he taught her to
- The empire's survival across the past decade traces almost entirely to his two-decade architecture of royal preservation: clandestine Osrid contact + daily Rhydia tutorship + own Vizier work + secret-keeping across all of it

**The hidden role re: Osrid:**
- **After Eldred's assassination 10 years before the game**, Umbra calculated that Osrid was the next assassination target as the last male heir. He **deliberately arranged Osrid's protective exile** — getting him out of the country under the cover of war service, judging that protecting Rhydia and Pierra was easier with Osrid removed from the country entirely.
- **Constructed the "Nomad Alban" cover identity** for Osrid using House Alban's quiet complicity (Section 2).
- **Maintained continuous clandestine contact with Osrid for the entire 10-year exile.** Communicated through some combination of Templar Arceus loyalist channels and other secret means.
- After Osrid was severely scarred in the Sevii Islands operation, **Umbra arranged Osrid's return to Cormoria** — bringing him home behind the Noh-styled Rainhawk mask.
- **In the first half of the game, Umbra remains the only person in Cormoria who knows Osrid is the prince.** Rhydia knows her brother is *alive* (Umbra told her years ago) but does not know he has *returned*. This is a deliberate Umbra calculation — *he is holding back the most personal information from his beloved pupil for strategic reasons.* The empire's safety requires Osrid's identity to remain concealed until the timing is right.

**The Umbra-Rhydia tension at the ~50% reveal:**

When Rhydia learns at ~50% through the game that "Rainhawk Alban" is her brother (during the joint Sword Cult encounter), **she realizes Umbra has been withholding this information from her** for the months Osrid has been back in Cormoria.

- Rhydia trusts Umbra more deeply than she trusts anyone else in her life. **He is the closest thing she has to a parent who is actually present.**
- *Umbra weighed his loyalty to the empire's strategic safety against his love for his pupil and chose the strategic calculus.* That is the kind of decision that haunts strategic geniuses — they make right decisions that injure the people they love.
- Rhydia *may* forgive him. She *may* not, at least not immediately. **Either way, the most trusting relationship in her life has now been complicated.** Umbra's strategic calculus has cost him something he cannot replace.

**The Umbra family Water specialty and the Jousteel line `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`:**

**House Umbra's noble-family Pokemon-type specialty is Water.** Water as the type of *reflection, depth, philosophy* — perfectly aligned with the Pokemon Philosopher identity. Water gives the family defensive coverage and access to the Steel/Fairy/Water meta the Cormorian aristocracy plays in.

**Cadmus Umbra's ace is Jousteel** (Water/Steel; see Section 9 — The Jousteel Line for full Pokemon data). Jousteel is *the* Pokemon species most closely identified with House Umbra — and uniquely so:

- The line is *not* a singular individual ace passed down generationally — it is a *bred lineage* the Umbra family maintains as a family project. Each Umbra raises their own Joustroll → Jousteel partnership.
- Cadmus's specific Jousteel is his lifelong partner, raised from his Joustroll days. Their bond is *long-term family-tradition deep.*

**Implications for Cormorian Pokemon ecology:**
- The Umbra family's cultivation of this line is a centuries-old responsibility

**The "three eggs" event in Cadmus's generation `[LOCKED v0.9.8]`:**

The Umbra-family Jousteel-line cultivation normally proceeds at a cadence of **one egg per generation** — a Jousteel produces a single egg in its long lifespan, that egg hatches into a Joustroll, the next-generation Umbra head raises that Joustroll into adulthood, and the line continues. Centuries of disciplined husbandry.

**Cadmus's generation produced an exceptional event:** the previous Jousteel laid **three eggs instead of one**. Two of the three hatched within the Umbra household — one became the **overworld attendant Joustroll** seen accompanying Cadmus around his laboratory; the other grew into his **personal ace Jousteel**. The third egg remained unhatched and was **sealed in a deep cavern accessible only to the Umbra family.**

The third egg is the hook for the Trial 4 Joustroll Egg sidequest. Full Joustroll/Jousteel species lore lives in **Section 9 — The Joustroll/Jousteel Line**, including the canonical Pokedex descriptions, stat lines, learnsets, evolution chain, and the *Cormorian engineered-weapons twin-pair* narrative connecting House Umbra's cultivation to the broader Cormorian-engineering thematic. **What Cadmus Umbra knows about Cormorian deep history `[LOCKED v0.9.4]`:**

**Cadmus Umbra does NOT know the deep cosmological truth of Cormoria's founding.** Despite being the most learned scholar in Cormoria, despite being the Pokemon Philosopher, despite tutoring Rhydia from infancy — he carries only the *fairy-tale-level* version of the founding myth:

- He knows that *Cormor Dream-Eater fended off a great evil in the past alongside the legendary beast Keerin, and founded the nation from that event.*
- He treats this as *probably-true-but-uncertain* historical record — consistent with his philosophical character. An honest scholar acknowledges uncertainty about ancient history.
- **He does not know:**
  - That the "great evil" has a name (Phancero) or what it actually was (Arceus's shadow-self made manifest)
  - That Keerin was a celestial partner rather than a tool Cormor used
  - That Cormor was originally a vengeance-driven tribesman rather than a heroic brigand
  - That the Treasures of Ruin are inverted Dreamstones / Cormor's original sealing implements
  - That Missingno is Phancero's first incursion
  - That Arceus's non-intervention is *active mercy with limits*

**This is critical character framing.** Even the most learned scholar in Cormoria is *not* the lore-keeper of the deep cosmology. The Blue Moon Hermit is the *last* of the nomadic-tribe oral-tradition lineage that preserved the actual history. **Cormoria's institutional knowledge has lost the truth across centuries** — Cadmus has only the cultivated mythology, like every other Cormorian scholar.

**This makes the player's late-game Book of the Moon discovery *more* dramatic.** Engaged players learn things *Cadmus Umbra himself does not know* — they reach the deepest layer of cosmological understanding that exists in the world. **The Book of the Moon contains knowledge that Cormoria's most learned scholar would *want* to know but has no path to.** This is a powerful reward for the engaged player.

### Silas Moray — Vizier of Commerce, the tragic-redemption antagonist `[LOCKED]`

**Silas Moray is one of the most thematically rich characters in the project — a tragic-redemption arc that elevates him from "secondary antagonist with a function" to "twisted father-figure who completes his arc through his death."**

**Family identity:**
- Head of **House Moray** — Psychic-type master family, longstanding Cormorian military tradition
- House Moray and House Nightfall are *natural type-rivals* (Psychic vs Dark) and *political rivals* (both compete for military influence)
- Silas's family's specialty is *mind* — psychic types, telepathy, foresight. The honorable use of this gift is *truth-revealing*. **Silas's perversion of it is mind-control and memory-erasure** — the same gift turned to violation.

**The deepest original wound — Domdaniel's rejection in childhood `[LOCKED]`:**

Before the Pierra-love wound, before Baradus's corruption, before any of it — **Silas was rejected as a child by House Moray's generational ace, Domdaniel** (the shiny Scream Tail with Huge Power; Section 2 — Generational Aces).

In Cormorian noble tradition, the family's generational Pokemon bonds with the heir. **Silas, the eldest Moray of his generation, expected to inherit Domdaniel. Domdaniel refused him.** We do not know the specifics — perhaps Domdaniel sensed something in young Silas that became the man he is now, perhaps the rejection happened *before* Silas's own moral compromise and *caused* some of it. **The Pokemon's judgment, in any case, was that this child was not worthy.**

This rejection has shaped Silas's entire psychological landscape:
- **His relationship to power has always been complicated by exclusion from the family's deepest power.** He is Head of House Moray but cannot wield the family's strongest asset.
- **His psychic powers are his own**, built from scratch — Thauma (the shiny Hisuian Braviary) and Malamar are his constructed arsenal, not his inheritance. **Without Domdaniel, he had to build his own power from nothing.** His Vizier-tier capability is genuinely earned.
- **The rejection by his family's sacred Pokemon prepared him for everything else.** Being told he wasn't good enough by Domdaniel made the later cosmic injustices — Pierra choosing Eldred, Eldred's assassination, Baradus's manipulation — feel like *continuations of a pattern that started in his childhood.* He has spent his life trying to be *enough*. He never was, in Domdaniel's judgment.
- **He has *never* spoken openly about Domdaniel's rejection.** This is the kind of wound a Moray would carry in silence. The Moray family knows; nobody outside the family does.

**The thematic weight of Silas's eventual redemption:**

When Silas pledges true loyalty to Osrid after his Vizier defeat and unmasking (Section 10 — Act V), and when he dies in the sealing cataclysm psychically constraining Baradus (Section 10 — Sealing Cataclysm), **he dies redeeming Domdaniel's judgment.** The Pokemon said he wasn't enough; he proved Domdaniel wrong in his final act. **The rejection was earned at the time but no longer accurate by the end. Silas dies having become someone Domdaniel would have chosen.**

This is tragic in the most precise sense. **The redemption arrives moments before the cost is paid.** And it is possible that Brie Moray — the niece Domdaniel did choose — recognizes this transformation in her uncle before he dies. `[OPEN]` whether Brie has any scene with Silas's death.

**The unrequited-love wound (the *second* original injury):**

After the Domdaniel rejection (and built on its foundation):
- Silas, Eldred Nightfall, and Pierra were of the same generational cohort, all in or adjacent to court
- **Both Eldred and Silas loved Pierra.** Eldred *acted* on those feelings and won her. They married. They had Osrid and Rhydia. Silas watched from outside the family he wanted his whole adult life.
- This unrequited-love wound is the *next* layer of injury — Silas had already been told by his family's Pokemon that he wasn't worthy; now the woman he loved chose his rival too. **The pattern Domdaniel started, Pierra continued.** Eldred's success felt like another judgment against Silas: that he was, somehow, simply *not enough*.
- Silas's grief at Eldred's assassination was complicated, mixing genuine sorrow with the secret shame of relief mixed with the public shame of grief.

**The mental breakdown and recruitment:**
- 10 years ago, Eldred is assassinated by the proto-Sword Cult (orchestrated by Baradus)
- Silas, complicated grief overwhelming him, has a mental breakdown
- **Baradus approaches him with a lie**: the Beads of Ruin killed Eldred, and the Sword of Ruin is the only weapon capable of destroying the Beads and avenging Eldred
- Silas, broken and grieving, accepts. He becomes 2IC of the Sword Cult.
- **Baradus deliberately conceals his own role in Eldred's death.** Silas does not know he is serving the man who killed his rival.

**The double delusion (the real psychological architecture):**

Silas now lives inside two layered lies:
1. **First delusion (Baradus's lie):** "The Beads of Ruin killed Eldred. The Sword of Ruin will avenge him. I am avenging my rival."
2. **Second delusion (self-imposed, beneath the first):** "I am helping Pierra heal. I am lifting the burden of rule off the woman I love. My manipulation is *care*."

The second delusion is more dangerous than the first because it is *self-administered*. Baradus put the first lie in his head. The *second* lie Silas built himself — a private moral fiction that reframes his violations of Pierra's mind as *love*. This is how he sleeps at night. This is how he looks Pierra in the eye each day. **He does not see what he is doing because he has convinced himself it is the opposite of what he is doing.**

**The twisted father-figure dynamic:**

Silas has watched Osrid and Rhydia grow up *as Vizier of Commerce*. He has seen them at court functions, advised on their education, been at family meals. And privately, unconsciously, he has thought: *"in another life these could have been my children. I am the one who stayed. They are almost mine."*

This is delusional possessiveness, a category of unsettling that has no clean name — neither romantic nor parental nor adversarial, but a delusion compounded of all three. Silas has constructed a *phantom-parenthood* in his mind across two decades.

**Critically:** Silas's pseudo-parental projection onto Osrid and Rhydia is *never acted on overtly.* He doesn't claim them. He doesn't say it. **The audience reads it through his behavior and dialogue, but it is never named aloud.** This restraint preserves the *privacy* of his delusion and the integrity of the tragedy.

**Silas's military service and intelligence operations:**
- Served as a senior officer in two international Cormorian campaigns
- Fought alongside both Eldred Nightfall and Nemo Korolev — **Silas and Nemo were comrades-in-arms under Eldred's command**
- Currently controls much of Cormoria's intelligence apparatus through Vizier of Commerce position
- Uses his psychic memory-erasure powers as a *forensic tool* — erasing memories of compromising encounters from witnesses, intelligence officers, and other state figures
- The intelligence apparatus is *corrupted* through Silas. This is why Nemo Korolev built a *parallel* private mercenary intelligence network to hunt the cults — he could not trust the official channels.

**Silas's aces and his use of his psychic powers:**

**Thauma** — shiny Hisuian Braviary (Psychic/Flying)
- Name from Greek θαῦμα (*thauma*, meaning *wonder*, *marvel*, *miracle*)
- The fact that Silas calls his manipulation-bird "Wonder" reveals how he conceives of his power: as **thaumaturgy**, sacred and beautiful, not as violation
- Hisuian Braviary's canonical lore involves it diving from great heights with psychic-amplified shouts that stun prey. **Silas attacks from above and through the mind** — exactly his character.
- Mechanical role: high-tier psychic damage and manipulation in battle
- Custom shiny palette planned (a more sinister color scheme to distinguish Silas's "perverted national bird" from the Templars' standard Braviary partners)
- See Section 14 (Production Discipline) for the pending custom shiny palette work for Thauma

**Malamar** — backup ace (Dark/Psychic)
- The Pokémon canonically associated with manipulation and control (Malamar's signature is Topsy-Turvy / Hypnosis-led psychological tactics)
- Likely runs sets focused on suppressive tactics: status conditions, stat-flipping, mental disruption

**Silas's memory-erasure mechanics (limits):**
- **Short-term memory only** — hours to days, NOT core identity memories. Silas cannot erase someone's whole life or fundamental sense of self.
- **Requires reinforcement** — erasures fade under sufficient emotional reinforcement. This is why his Pierra-manipulation is *ongoing* and *daily* — he has to keep maintaining it, not set-and-forget.
- This limit also explains why Silas does not just erase Osrid's memories on first encounter. The cost-benefit isn't worth it for a brief encounter; only sustained presence allows manipulation to take hold.
- See Section 9 — Key Artifacts and Mechanics for the full mechanic note.

**Silas's hostility to the Beads Cult (driven by the lie):**
- Silas genuinely believes the Beads Cult killed Eldred (Baradus's planted lie)
- He is *actively hostile* to Beads Cult members, **including Eden** when their paths cross
- This creates dramatic tension when Eden's defection to Osrid's side puts her in proximity to Silas — Silas sees Eden as a member of the cult he hates, not as Osrid's ally
- `[OPEN]` Whether Eden and Silas have any direct confrontation scene

**The redemption arc (FULL):**

**The mid-game and late-game Silas beats:**

1. **Silas as Vizier of Commerce battle (one of the 4 Viziers)** — boss fight with Thauma and Malamar as aces
2. **After defeat, Osrid unmasks** — Silas's reaction-dialogue *implies* his pseudo-parental recognition without stating it (audience reads it, no character names it)
3. **Osrid/Umbra reveal the Baradus-killed-Eldred truth** — Silas learns that he has been serving the man who killed his rival and orchestrated the original wound that broke him
4. **Silas pledges true loyalty** to Osrid — not just because of the Baradus revelation, but because **the prince he privately considered his own son has come home and revealed himself to him.** In Silas's private cosmology, this is the moment he has been waiting for without knowing it. *The prodigal son has come home, and Silas, in his delusion, was always going to serve that boy when he returned.* This is the most coherent thing he has ever done.
5. **Silas serves Osrid through the final act** — likely providing intel on Baradus's operations, possibly helping locate Nemo's captivity (Section 7 — Nemo), possibly other operational support
6. **Final battle, Silas's death:** Silas uses his **psychic powers to constrain Baradus** and **destroy the resonance bond** that Chien-Pao is forcing on Baradus. The backlash of *destroying the resonance + the energy released as a byproduct of sealing all 4 Treasures at once is too much for humans to channel.* **Silas dies from the psychic backlash.** (Osrid loses his memories from the same overload — see Section 10 — Story Spine.)

**The thematic resolution of Silas's death:**
- His **psychic gift** (his family's hereditary specialty, which he abused with Pierra) is *redeemed by use* in service of protecting Cormoria
- His **service to Baradus** is reversed *in the same moment of action* — the man he served as 2IC, he now binds
- His **death** is the *cost* of using his power at maximum capacity
- **Pierra is still protected** by Silas's actions, but indirectly — by the sealing of the Treasures that would otherwise threaten everything, including her. **He dies enabling the protection his decades-long manipulation always claimed to be.** What was instrumentally a lie becomes literally true at the end. The shape of his redemption is the discovery that the role he played in delusion can finally be played for real.
- **Silas's death is the only uncoerced act of love he ever performed.** He couldn't be Pierra's husband; he couldn't be Osrid's father. So in his final moments he is both, briefly — protecting Pierra and serving Osrid simultaneously.
- Pierra survives the epilogue with Rhydia. The woman two men loved outlives them both.

**Team `[LOCKED v0.9.7 — aces; rest of team OPEN]`:**

The name "Temperance" invokes the Christian cardinal virtue (self-restraint, moderation, balance). **A Dark-typed Pokemon as the Archbishop's ace is a layered statement** — possibly that *Arceus's order is willing to wield Dark as a tool in defense of the world*, possibly that *Ambrose himself carries some moral darkness* that complicates his public role. **Or both true at once** (this is consistent with the project's emotional register).

**The three forces pulling at Ambrose `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

Ambrose is not corrupted by the cults, but he is *not aligned with the crown* either. He has been paralyzed by indecision for years because three distinct forces pull him in different directions:

1. **Sword Cult pressure via Silas Moray:** Baradus, through Silas, has been pressuring and manipulating Templar order members for several years. **Some bishops under Ambrose's authority are secretly cult-aligned** and whisper conflicting information into his ear about imperial politics, the royal family's true motives, and the moral standing of the crown. Ambrose can no longer fully trust his own subordinates — and *that loss of trust in his immediate institutional family* is itself a form of cult damage.

2. **Genuine personal relationship with Cadmus Umbra:** Ambrose and Umbra are on **very good terms** through decades of religious-civil cooperation. Umbra has been candid with Ambrose about imperial matters within the bounds of state secrecy; Ambrose has provided religious-institutional support to Umbra's various initiatives. *This is a pull toward the crown.*

3. **Deep formative friendship with Nemo Korolev:** When Nemo arrived in Cormoria as a Sinnohian war-orphan and grew up in and around the Templar order, **he became close friends with the young Ambrose.** The friendship persisted into adulthood — even as Nemo rose to Dawnstar and then stepped down to the 9th Trial Baron seat, even as Ambrose rose to Archbishop, they remained close adult friends across decades. **Of all Ambrose's relationships, this is the deepest.** *And this is the pull that Silas's lie targets.*

**The Silas lie about Nemo `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

At some point in the early-to-mid game, **Silas — under Baradus's orders — tells Ambrose a calculated lie about Nemo's career:** that *"Nemo was forced to become a Trial Baron by the crown as punishment for rejecting the empire's demands that he commit atrocities overseas for the Cormorian military."*

**The truth (already locked Section 7 — Nemo):** Nemo *voluntarily* stepped down from Dawnstar to negotiate the 9th Trial Baron seat himself, in exchange for the operational obscurity he needed to run his private cult-hunting intelligence network.

**The lie's psychology — surgical precision targeting Ambrose's vulnerabilities:**
- The lie frames the *crown* as the aggressor against Nemo (Ambrose's beloved friend) — which is the *one* claim that could turn the Archbishop against Pierra and Rhydia
- It positions Nemo as the *moral hero* who refused atrocities, which is *fully consistent with Ambrose's existing view of his friend* (Nemo is the kind of man who would refuse atrocities)
- It exploits Ambrose's *love for Nemo* to turn that love into anti-crown sentiment
- **Silas can deliver this credibly because Silas served alongside Nemo under Eldred for years.** The man telling the lie has the documented military relationship with the supposed victim that makes the lie *plausible*

**Silas's calculation is masterful and the cruelest specific act of his cult-aligned career.** He served beside Nemo. They were comrades-in-arms. Silas knows the truth about Nemo's voluntary retirement deal (he was Vizier of Commerce during the negotiation, part of the apparatus). **And Silas chose to weaponize that knowledge to corrupt Nemo's other closest friend.**

**Holding a Mega-Dreamstone shard `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

Ambrose secretly possesses **one of the 9 scattered Mega-Dreamstone shards** (Section 9 — The Mega-Dreamstone Shard Quest). He has been holding it for years, unsure whom to entrust it to:

- The crown might use it to consolidate power *or* might fail to protect it from the cults
- The cults are after it but Ambrose understands them well enough to know they would weaponize it
- The Templar order itself contains cult-aligned bishops Ambrose cannot trust
- **There is no one Ambrose can fully trust.** And so he holds it himself, alone, and the holding is eating him alive.

**He is in near emotional hysteria internally** — though he holds it together publicly because an Archbishop visibly hysterical would precipitate a political crisis. The shard's weight (literal and figurative) is *compounding* his torn-three-ways indecision. *He cannot give it to the wrong side, and he cannot identify the right side.*

**The fight context — the bishop-coup foil `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:**

In the second half of the game, the **cult-aligned bishops under Ambrose's authority attempt a coup** — they move to seize the Templar apparatus from Ambrose, which would deliver empire-wide religious-civic infrastructure to the Sword Cult (the Tavern network, the Braviary travel system, the order's intelligence-gathering, the institutional moral authority). **Osrid intervenes and foils the coup, fighting off the cult-aligned bishops.**

At that moment, Ambrose — who has been paralyzed by indecision for years — decides that he needs to **fight Osrid himself** as the way to *"get to the truth of things."* His coping mechanism for years of paralysis becomes a single-combat moral test:

**For a Templar of the Arceus order — whose entire theology rests on partnership as cosmic-sacred — combat is a form of moral revelation.** Ambrose cannot tell through political argument who is telling him the truth. But he *can* judge a man through how that man fights and bonds with his Pokemon. **Ambrose is using the Pokemon-as-truth framework to break his own paralysis.**
