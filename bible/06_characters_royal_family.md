## 7. Major NPCs

### The Royal Family of Cormoria `[LOCKED]`

**Naming convention:** The matrilineal Cormoria line is the actual royal line. Spouses retain their birth-house names and append "Cormoria" — so the dynasty is preserved through the female line. Children take the Cormoria surname directly.

**Empress Pierra Cormoria I** — the woman two men loved, the woman who outlives them both `[LOCKED]`
- Direct descendant of the Cormoria royal line; the founder-monarch of this dynastic branch (hence "I")
- Mother of Osrid and Rhydia
- **Was loved by two men in her generation: Eldred Nightfall and Silas Moray.** Eldred won her; they married and had two children. Silas watched from outside the family he wanted. This unrequited-love wound is the *original* injury in Silas Moray's life and the predicate for everything that comes after (Section 7 — Silas).
- Relied heavily on her late husband Eldred for political strategy and empire management
- After Eldred's assassination she became gradually overwhelmed by grief and **retreated to the royal Secret Villa** (Section 2 — Royal Secret Villa) — a secluded family estate physically far from the imperial capital and *geographically near House Moray's estate.* She has lived in self-imposed seclusion there for most of the 10 years since Eldred's assassination.
- **The geographic detail is the manipulation infrastructure:** Pierra is essentially Silas Moray's neighbor at the Villa. This is how Silas's daily psychic manipulation has been logistically possible — he can visit her on a near-daily basis to maintain his memory-erasure manipulation reinforcement. Pierra retreated to the Villa for safety and privacy after her husband's death; in doing so, she unknowingly placed herself in *the worst possible location for her emotional safety*. The geography is the trap.
- **Has been long-manipulated by Silas Moray** through psychic memory-influence (short-term memory erasure, sustained over years, reinforced repeatedly — see Section 9 — Silas's memory-erasure mechanics)
- Silas's manipulation is dressed in his *own* private self-deception that it is "care" — he believes he is *helping* Pierra by lifting the burden of rule. He sees himself as a kind of pseudo-husband fulfilling the role Eldred had. He sees Osrid and Rhydia as the children he never had. **This is delusion compounded with violation, but Silas does not see it as violation.** From Pierra's perspective, she is being subtly steered by her trusted Vizier of Commerce in ways she does not perceive.
- Not a villain. A grieving widow whose loneliness made her susceptible to a long-game manipulator who genuinely (in his twisted self-conception) believed himself her caretaker.
- **Survives the epilogue, mourning her son as dead.** Outlives Eldred (assassinated 10 years before story). Outlives Silas (dies in the final-battle sealing cataclysm, Section 10). **Believes Osrid died in the sealing cataclysm.** *She does not learn that he survived.* He chooses not to return; he never sends word; she lives the rest of her life believing she lost him twice — first to the false news 10 years earlier, then for real in the cataclysm. **She lives on with Rhydia, her surviving daughter**, presiding over a partially-collapsed but enduring Cormorian nobility. The classical tragedy of her arc has its fullest weight: she is loved by two men in her life, both of whom die, and her surviving son chooses freedom over reunion.
- **Authorized Nemo Korolev's surname exception** — the only known case in Cormorian history of a non-royal-bestowed surname being acknowledged as legitimate by the Cormorian state (Section 2). The fact that she did so reveals her as *not* a rigid traditionalist; she has at least once in her reign authorized an unprecedented action against ancient tradition. She is a grieving widow, yes, but she is also capable of pragmatic governance when circumstances call for it.
- The classical tragedy of her arc: she is the central beloved figure of the project, and she ends the story alone except for one daughter, having been loved by two men whose love became weapons aimed at others (Eldred's tactical mind, Silas's psychic manipulation).

**Prince-Consort Eldred Nightfall Cormoria** (deceased)
- Descendant of **House Nightfall**, one of the five-oldest noble houses (Section 12); masters of Dark-types
- House Nightfall has a long bloodline of military commanders and intelligence officers
- Incredibly skilled tactician; rose to the top of the Cormorian military
- **`[EXPANDED v0.9.8]` Eldred's background and rise:** Eldred was the **second and youngest son of Mordred Nightfall.** His older brother **Dantalion "Dan" Nightfall** was the chosen family heir, tutored by the best trainers and groomed for prominence, while Eldred was enrolled in military school in his early teens. Eldred became a **completely self-taught Dark-type specialist** after being drafted into formal military service at age 15. He became the **youngest person in Cormorian history to advance past the Rainhawk military rank to Tarasque before the age of 20.** When he returned from service, he **defeated his tutored older brother Dantalion in a highly-marketed public match** — and, combined with his military reputation, became *the face of House Nightfall* and a *better Dark-type trainer than his own brother the Trial Baron.* Dantalion was humiliated and went into seclusion (Section 12 — Nightfall family tree). Eldred subsequently married Pierra (winning her over his childhood-friend-and-rival Silas Moray; Section 7 — Silas) and became Emperor-Consort.
- Married Pierra in his early adulthood and took the Cormoria surname (in addition to keeping his House surname)
- **Prince-Consort, not King** — Pierra is the actual heir of the Cormoria line
- **Assassinated by Baradus (Sword Cult / proto-Widening-Eye) during Osrid and Rhydia's childhood (10 years before game start, when the twins were 10)** — this was the *opening move* of the cults' long political campaign against Cormoria. The cults removed the tactical anchor first; the rest of their infiltration followed. **Eldred's assassination was also the catalyst that splintered the Widening Eye into the four competing Ruin Cults and triggered the rise of the Swords of Ruin** (Section 8 — cult origins, v0.9.8 catalyst lock).
- `[LOCKED]` The "unknown party" the public believes assassinated him remains officially unknown. The player learns the truth as part of the late-game revelation.

**The extended Nightfall family `[LOCKED v0.9.8 — full tree in Section 12]`:**
- **Mordred Nightfall** — Eldred's father, the Nightfall patriarch. Former Trial Baron (10 years), then **Champion of Cormoria for 50 years** until defeated by his own 15-year-old granddaughter Rhydia 5 years before game start. **Childhood friend and lifelong rival of Falco Raptora** (Section 7 — Falco); the two were considered the strongest trainers of their generation, both with immense vitality. Mordred's 50-year Champion tenure wore him down — his loss to Rhydia was partly weariness and a possibly-subconscious readiness to pass the baton (his peak skill was so immense that even the best 15-year-old in the world could not have beaten him at full power). Retired to the Nightfall estate after the defeat. *Bridget feels closest to Mordred of all her family.*
- **Dantalion "Dan" Nightfall** — Eldred's older brother, the once-chosen heir. Humiliated by Eldred's public defeat of him; went into seclusion; after Eldred's assassination, carrying guilt/trauma/humiliation/love/sadness/hatred, **fled into diplomatic service as Cormoria's envoy to Kalos.** Never appears on-screen; referenced in dialogue only.
- **Leah Alban Nightfall** — Dantalion's wife, married into House Nightfall from **House Alban** (the Grass-type Trial 6 House). Neglected by the secluded Dantalion and emotionally unstable; largely absent during her daughter's upbringing. *The Alban-Nightfall marriage is one example of how deeply Cormoria's old Houses have intermarried over time.*
- **Bridget Nightfall** — Dantalion and Leah's daughter; Osrid and Rhydia's first cousin; born exactly 10 years before the twins (age 30 at game start). **The 7th Trial Baroness (Dark-type), matriarch-by-default of declining House Nightfall.** Spent the 10 years after Eldred's assassination as an older-sister figure to the young Rhydia (alongside Cadmus Umbra). See Section 7 — Bridget Nightfall for the full entry.

**Prince Osrid Cormoria** — protagonist, elder twin, first of his name
- Inherits the Cormoria royal line through Pierra
- Inherits House Nightfall blood through Eldred — bears ancestral Dark-type affinity
- Sent off to war as a young teenager
- Earned the rank of Rainhawk
- Heir-presumptive by primogeniture before his presumed death; constitutionally ambiguous after his return
- House Nightfall's Dark-type tradition makes Osrid's bloodline a **refutation** of the cults' worldview: even Dark-types — the typing of the Treasures themselves — can be true partners rather than weapons

**Queen-Regent Rhydia Cormoria** — younger twin, first of her name, Champion of Cormoria
- **See the full Rhydia profile below** in this Section for complete documentation.
- Brief summary: 20 years old, slightly younger twin of Osrid; tutored from infancy by Cadmus Umbra; effectively ruling Cormoria for 10 years while Pierra is in seclusion at the Villa; Champion of Cormoria with the strongest known trainer-bonded Pokemon in the empire (Justice the Iron Valiant, plus shiny Drampa Puff); **her first major act as acting Regent-Empress was at age 12 (8 years pre-game): issuing the formal decree recognizing Kimaris Langerin's voluntary renunciation of the Champion seat in favor of the Trial Baron seat and Langerin patriarchy** (the only-just-Regent-age twelve-year-old issuing a structural empire decree was itself a politically extraordinary event; Section 7 — Kimaris Langerin v0.9.8); **won the Champion seat 5 years before story start by defeating her own grandfather Mordred Nightfall — the 50-year reigning Champion — when she was 15** (Section 12 — Nightfall family tree; Mordred's loss was partly weariness after 50 years, but Rhydia's victory was real and cemented her reputation as exceptional); two years before story start defeated Kimaris Langerin's Marshadow in a public Champion's-defense match (Kimaris's *Reserve-Champion-tests-the-reigning-Champion* deployment, locked v0.9.8); idolized by the citizenry; missing her brother daily; **the only person besides Cadmus Umbra who knew Osrid was alive during his 10-year exile**, though she does not know he has returned at game start. **Her first cousin Bridget Nightfall served as an older-sister figure to her across the 10 years after Eldred's assassination** (alongside Umbra), though at game start there is distance and unspoken negativity tangled with real love between the cousins (Section 7 — Bridget Nightfall).

**Constitutional shadow:** Osrid is the elder twin and was heir-presumptive. His return creates a quiet, unaddressed question — should he resume the role? Osrid's character resolves this by *not wanting* it. His arc is finding new purpose outside the prince identity. He quietly cedes any claim. Rhydia is functionally Empire's leader, and Osrid prefers it that way.

**Generational legacy split:**
- Rhydia's biological line continues the *Cormorian royal* legacy (which probably ends with the empire's eventual collapse)
- Osrid's adopted line (the Amber-Letz/Berlitz family) produces Volo and Cynthia — the more *historically significant* descendants from a modern Pokémon-canon perspective
- Sister and brother, both lost to history in different ways

### Queen-Regent Rhydia Cormoria — the empire's pride, the reigning Champion, the hidden prince's twin `[LOCKED — header corrected v0.9.8]`

**Rhydia is one of the largest and most fully-realized characters in the project — the strongest Pokemon trainer in the empire, the de facto ruler for the past decade, and the missing-piece-keeper for her exiled brother.**

**Identity and family:**
- 20 years old; slightly younger twin of Osrid (Osrid is the elder by minutes)
- Daughter of Empress Pierra Cormoria I and the late Prince-Consort Eldred Nightfall
- Inherits the Cormoria royal line through Pierra and House Nightfall through Eldred
- Champion of Cormoria — the strongest Champion in generations; first sub-30 Champion in over 1000 years
- Heir to House Nightfall through her father (the house is currently in leadership question after Eldred's death)
- Carries the royal "Cormoria" surname directly through matrilineal inheritance

**Tutored by Cadmus Umbra from infancy `[LOCKED]`:**
- **Vizier Cadmus Umbra has been Rhydia's tutor since she was a baby** — essentially a third parent in her life alongside Pierra (and Eldred, until his death)
- Umbra's careful strategic mind is embedded in everything Rhydia has been taught: politics, battling, governance, philosophy, history, statesmanship
- Over 20 years, Umbra has poured every aspect of his strategic capability into shaping her
- **Rhydia is in some sense Umbra's life work.** She is what he made when he had two decades to make her.
- **Umbra has been simultaneously preparing both royal children for their respective roles** — clandestine contact with Osrid overseas + daily tutorship of Rhydia at home. The empire's continued survival traces almost entirely to Umbra's two-decade architecture of royal preservation.
- *Rhydia trusts Umbra more deeply than she trusts anyone else in her life.* When Umbra eventually reveals that he kept Osrid's return secret from her until ~50%, this creates a *real* tension between them — Rhydia may forgive him; she may have complicated feelings about it; either way, *Umbra weighed his loyalty to the empire's strategic safety against his love for Rhydia and chose the strategic calculus.* That is the kind of decision that haunts strategic geniuses — they make right decisions that injure the people they love.

**The 10-year regency:**
- **At age 10, Rhydia's world collapsed.** Her father was assassinated. Her elder twin was sent away by Umbra "for his protection." Her mother retreated into grief and seclusion at the royal Secret Villa near House Moray's estate (Section 2 — Royal Secret Villa).
- **From approximately age 10 onward, Rhydia gradually took over her mother's rule.** No formal abdication ever occurred — Pierra is still technically the Empress — but Rhydia has been doing the actual work of governance for the past decade.
- The Trial Barons, the Viziers, and the broader Cormorian state apparatus have accepted Rhydia's de facto leadership. **She has been the empire's actual head of state since at least her late teens.**
- She is *not* a figurehead. **She is essentially the only reason the empire hasn't fully fallen to Baradus and Goma's schemes for the past decade.** The cults' gradual infiltration has been slowed and resisted by her single-handed political work — exposing infiltrators, replacing corrupted officials, recruiting loyal personnel, intercepting cult correspondence, maintaining popular legitimacy.
- **But it is a losing battle.** The cult infiltration is accelerating faster than she can resist. The Sword Cult is gradually corrupting noble houses, **the Nobility Concordance**, merchant guilds like **Sylphon & Stone**, and even the **Templar Arceus halls**. *She is doing the work of many; she is still just one young woman.*

**Personality — brilliant, exhausted, lonely, popular:**
- **Brilliant:** Extremely quick study on things she doesn't know. Politically savvy. Genuinely understands policy and good statesmanship. **Excellent at delegation** — she has built networks of capable people across Cormoria who handle work she trusts them with. Her competence is *not* solo-heroism; it is *organizational genius* that multiplies her own efforts.
- **Exhausted:** She has been doing this for 10 years. She is 20 years old. *She is tired.* The cult crisis is accelerating; her capacity is failing. Osrid's return at game start arrives at the moment her solo capacity can no longer hold. **She does not yet know that help is coming.**
- **Lonely:** Her father is dead; her mother is in seclusion miles away and barely present; her brother is overseas (per Umbra's partial truth she has been told); her tutor Umbra is her closest confidant but is also her *teacher*, not her peer. **She has no equals in her own generation that she can fully trust.** The closest she has to a peer is *the public*, whose love she returns by being constantly present among them.
- **Missing her brother daily:** Rhydia thinks about Osrid every day. Umbra told her years ago that Osrid was alive, just not in Cormoria. She assumes he is still overseas as the game begins. She does not know he has returned under the Nomad Alban cover identity. **At game start, she walks past her own brother repeatedly in early-game scenes without knowing.** She has been mourning his absence for a decade while he has been right there in plain sight, masked.
- **Popular:** **"The nation's pride and joy."** Often described to the population as a princess who actually mingles with the people and is on the front lines doing actual transparent work. She is *idolized* by most of the citizens and is far more popular than anyone else in the empire. Her popular legitimacy is *earned through visibility*, not granted by ceremony.

**The twin-mirror dynamic with Osrid `[cross-reference Section 5]`:**

Osrid and Rhydia responded to the same family trauma (their father's assassination at age 10) in *opposite* ways. **Osrid went inward** — masks, false identities, concealment, becoming a smaller and smaller visible self. **Rhydia went outward** — public service, popular engagement, transparent governance, becoming the most visible figure in the empire. **The twins are mirror images of each other in their relationship to identity.**

When they reunite at ~50% (Rhydia learns Osrid is the new Rainhawk), they begin in some sense learning from each other about how to inhabit royal identity. Osrid teaches her about the cost of visibility; Rhydia teaches him about the courage of being seen.

**Public-facing Champion combat record:**
- Has demonstrated her unbelievable battling prowess numerous times in public
- Has bested **Dawnstar-level trainers** in exhibition matches and political-stakes battles
- **Two years before the game's story starts, Rhydia defeated Kimaris Langerin** — the 1st Trial Baron, **Reserve Champion** of Cormoria (Section 7 — Kimaris Langerin v0.9.8 lock; not "former" — never held the seat), head of House Langerin — **with his full Reserve-Champion-tier team, including House Langerin's most special Pokemon inheritance: a level 100 Marshadow as his ace.**
  - This was *not* a Trial battle (which is at level cap 18 for Kimaris). It was an exhibition or political-stakes match between the reigning Champion (Rhydia) and the **Reserve Champion** (Kimaris) at *unrestricted* levels. Section 7 — Kimaris Langerin (v0.9.8 lock) — Reserve, not former.
  - **Rhydia won.** This victory cemented her Championship legitimacy beyond doubt and triggered her surge in popularity over the past two years.
  - The Cormorian public stopped seeing her as "the Regent-Queen who took her father's place" and started seeing her as "the Champion who can beat anyone, including the Reserve Champion at full power."
  - **This victory is also what makes Marshadow a known existence in cult intelligence** — the cults learned that House Langerin has a Mythical-tier ace, and they have factored this into their long-term strategy.

**Rhydia's full team `[LOCKED]`:**

**Two permanent members:**

1. **Justice — the royal heirloom Iron Valiant**
   - **Species:** Iron Valiant (Paradox Pokemon, shiny variant)
   - **In-world identity:** A uniquely-evolved demigod form of Ralts, discovered in a metal chamber by Cormor Dream-Eater centuries ago, the royal family's bonded ace across generations (Section 2 — Justice subsection)
   - **Ability:** Sharpness (boosts slicing moves by 50%)
   - **Moveset:** Sacred Sword, Tachyon Cutter, Bitter Blade, Mighty Cleave (all four are slicing moves boosted by Sharpness)

   - **Combat profile:** Choice Scarf + base 116 Speed = 174 effective Speed, outspeeding nearly everything in the game. Four-type STAB-equivalent coverage (Fighting/Steel/Fire/Rock) hits virtually every defensive typing in the game for neutral or super-effective. Bitter Blade provides self-healing. Mighty Cleave ignores screens and Protect. **There is no clean type-counter to Justice.** It is the strongest known Pokemon currently bonded to a trainer in all of Cormoria.

2. **Puff — Rhydia's childhood starter**
   - **Species:** Drampa (shiny lineage variant)
   - **Personal significance:** Rhydia's *childhood starter*. The bond goes back to before her father's death. **Puff is one of the few constants in Rhydia's life across 20 years.** Puff was there when she was a happy princess; Puff was there when her father died; Puff has been there through her solo regency.
   - **Ability:** Multiscale (halves damage taken at full HP — rare, extremely powerful)
   - **Moveset:** Dragon Energy (signature Dragon move, 150 BP scaling with HP), Hyper Beam (150 BP Normal, recharge turn), Thunderclap (Electric priority — **Ruination-added learnset modification**; Drampa does not canonically learn Thunderclap, this is Puff's unique inheritance move), Flash Cannon (Steel coverage)
   - **Item:** Assault Vest (+50% Sp.Def, no status moves)
   - **Combat profile:** Multiscale + Assault Vest = enormous opening survivability. Dragon Energy at full HP fires at 150 BP under Multiscale protection. **Puff is the strongest known Dragon under a trainer's command in the whole Empire** despite Drampa's modest BST — Multiscale + Assault Vest + the elite moveset compensate dramatically.

**Cycling team members (rotated based on opponent):**

- **Swampert** (Water/Ground) — bulky physical attacker, Electric immunity
- **Zebstrika** (Electric) — speedy Electric attacker, counter to Water/Flying

**Strategic intelligence:**
- Rhydia *scouts opponents* and adjusts her cycling team before the Champion fight. She approaches her team as a craftsman approaches their tools — always improving, always testing.
- **She is constantly training a new iteration** of her cycling team; the members listed above are her *current* roster but she rotates new candidates in over time.
- **The Champion fight is *intelligent*** — not just hard mechanically, but adapted specifically to whatever team the player brings.

**The doubles-format preview subplot `[LOCKED — major narrative beat]`:**

After Trial 2 (Reid Ashland / Fire), during the **Goma assassination arc** (Section 8 — Goma; Section 10 — Story Spine), the player participates in a few **double battles** alongside Rhydia. Opponents are **low-level Beads Cult acolytes** and **one Bishop** (the shorthand name for a mid-tier Beads Cult trainer, usually leading a small group of acolytes).

**Critical setup:** Rhydia explicitly says during this event that she has **left Puff and Justice at home** — she is doing field-work against minor cult agents and brought only the lower-level Pokemon she is in the process of fully training for her new team iteration.

**Player intelligence outcome:**
- Player sees *some* of Rhydia's cycling team in live combat — enough to know she has variety, enough to begin thinking about counters
- Player does **not** see Justice or Puff (the two permanent aces remain mysteries)
- Player can build counter-strategy but cannot fully solve the Champion battle in advance
- This respects the player's intelligence while preserving the boss-fight surprise

**Cooperative-then-adversarial relationship structure:**
- The player and Rhydia *fight on the same side* in the early game (the doubles assassination subplot)
- They get to know each other's battling style through *cooperation*, not combat
- When the player eventually fights Rhydia as Champion at the end of Act V, they are fighting someone they have *fought alongside* — the emotional weight of opposition is built on prior partnership
- **This is the opposite of most fan-game rivalries** (which establish opposition first and then sometimes evolve into respect). The player-Rhydia relationship is built on respect first, then becomes opposition through circumstance (Champion vs challenger), then resolves through revelation (sister vs brother).

**The Champion battle at the climax of Act V `[LOCKED]`:**

Rhydia is the *final trainer-tier opponent* the player faces before the Hall of Fame unmasking (Section 5, Section 10). She brings her full team — Justice + Puff + cycling members selected based on what she has observed about the player's team across the game.

**The emotional charge of this fight:**
- Rhydia has already learned at ~50% that "Rainhawk Alban" is her brother. She has fought alongside him through Acts II-V knowing the truth she cannot publicly state.
- **The Champion battle is brother vs sister, with both knowing the truth, neither yet ready for the public reveal.**
- Puff in particular carries family-recognition weight — Puff knew Osrid as a child. When Puff comes out, Osrid is meeting his childhood again. **Puff fighting Osrid is Rhydia's childhood fighting Osrid's adulthood.** Twin recognition through a shared bond animal.
- Justice — the royal heirloom — is *Rhydia's* possession but represents *the dynasty itself*. When Osrid defeats Justice, he is in some sense defeating the institutional Cormoria he is about to become Regent-King of. **The fight is both deeply personal and deeply political.**

**Post-battle:**
- Rhydia loses. The empire formally acknowledges Osrid as the new Champion. The Hall of Fame unmasking sequence follows (Section 5).
- **Rhydia retains House Nightfall leadership and remains a major figure in the post-coronation political order**, even as Osrid claims the Regent-King + Champion roles.
- After the sealing cataclysm at the end of Act VI, **Rhydia believes Osrid died.** She does not learn he survived. She continues as Champion in the post-game, presiding over a Cormoria that mourns its lost Regent-King forever (Section 10 — Epilogue).

**Why Rhydia matters thematically:**

Rhydia is the *active* refutation of the project's design temptation toward "the prince saves the empire." **The empire does not need saving by a returned prince — it has been being saved every day by his sister for a decade.** Osrid's return is not the empire's salvation; it is *Rhydia's relief*. The most exhausted hero in the project is the one who has been doing the most work, alone, for the longest. **She is the project's quiet protagonist running parallel to the player's protagonist.**

She is also the embodiment of the *cost* of the cult crisis — not as a victim but as the one who has been holding the line. **Every day the cult crisis has continued, Rhydia has been paying for it in exhaustion, isolation, and forgone youth.** A 20-year-old who has been the empire's de facto ruler for 10 years has paid more than the surface of the story shows. Her popularity is real; her capability is real; her loneliness and tiredness are *also* real.

And finally, **Rhydia's continued existence after the epilogue is the project's quiet hope for Cormoria.** Osrid renounces and leaves. The cult crisis ends with cataclysmic cost. Pierra mourns alone. But **Rhydia continues**, governing what remains, holding the country together as she always has. The project does not give us a triumphant restoration of the empire. It gives us *Rhydia carrying on*. That is enough.

**Rhydia's overworld ace-bonding interactions — the two-ace structure `[LOCKED v0.9.8 — narrative; engineering OPEN]`:**

Per Section 11 — Ace-Bonding Overworld Interactions, Rhydia qualifies for the pattern with **two named aces deployed in parallel**, both present in her overworld appearances. This is the project's *original* two-ace structure (Section 11 — pattern); Ambrose Caymen (Temperance + Brunhilda) and Sable Ashland (Solara + Gouging Fire) follow this same pattern locked in v0.9.8.

- **Justice (shiny Iron Valiant, Sharpness ability) — the Champion-tier combat ace.** The royal heirloom Iron Valiant whose existence directly shapes Baradus's three-fears framework (Section 8 — Baradus's fears). Interaction tone: **earned-respect, slightly-formal partnership.** Justice is not Rhydia's pet; Justice is *the empire's living weapon entrusted to its Champion*, and Rhydia carries that weight visibly in how she addresses the Iron Valiant. Sample register: *"Justice, with me,"* *"Ready,"* — short, military-precise, *equal-partner* rather than commander-to-tool. **The interaction makes legible to the player that Rhydia respects the Iron Valiant's lineage and historical role**, not just its combat utility.
- **Puff (shiny Drampa, Multiscale ability) — the personal-companion ace.** Drampa is canonically a gentle, child-loving species; Rhydia's Puff is *visibly her personal partner* in a way Justice cannot quite be, since Justice carries the empire's weight. Interaction tone: **unguarded affection.** Rhydia is *visibly relaxed* around Puff in a way she rarely is around anyone or anything else. Sample register: *"Hello, sweetheart,"* a hand stroking Puff's mustache, a soft conversational murmur. **Puff is where Rhydia gets to be twenty years old again** — where the exhausted Queen-Regent / Champion can briefly *not* be either.
- **The two-ace contrast is itself character:** Rhydia carries empire-weight in her relationship with Justice; she gets to be a person in her relationship with Puff. **Both are real, both are the same Rhydia, both are necessary for her to survive what she carries.** Engaged players observe this contrast and understand more about Rhydia's exhaustion than she ever articulates aloud.
- **The relationship to Osrid's eventual return:** when Osrid is finally back in Rhydia's life as her brother, *Puff is the first member of the royal household Rhydia introduces him to.* Justice gets introduced through the Hall of Fame moment when Osrid sees Rhydia in combat at her Champion-tier; Puff gets a separate quieter introduction. **Puff is family-private; Justice is empire-public.** The distinction is part of how Rhydia signals to Osrid that she has *both* lives, and that her continued duty to the empire (Justice) does not exhaust her capacity to receive him as a brother (Puff).
