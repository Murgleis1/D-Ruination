## 3. Geopolitics — The World in Our Era

### Cormoria (our setting) `[LOCKED]`

- **Meritocratic empire.** Commoners can rise through military, business, or academia to become Viziers. Has nobility and royalty but isn't *defined* by them.
- **Wealth and stability** — the richest, most powerful nation of our era.
- **Court structure:** Queen-Regent + four Viziers + nine noble Trial Houses + military hierarchy capped by the rank of Tarasque.
- **Cultural values:** meritocracy, altruism, Pokémon-philosophy over Pokémon-as-weaponry.
- **Currently vulnerable** — the cults have spent years infiltrating its institutions.

### Paldea (Eden's homeland) `[LOCKED]`

- **Recently founded empire in consolidation phase** — Eden's "kingdom" is the imperial core.
- **Rigid two-tier caste society** — peasant or royal/noble, no middle. Caste is birth-determined.
- **Currently the country with the closest formal relationship to Cormoria**, but the relationship is asymmetric — Paldea is poor, divided, and resents Cormorian prosperity.
- **Serfs cannot attend school or learn to read** unless they are born into merchant or noble class.
- **The current king is a pivotal figure** — a "greedy ignorant nepotic trust-fund baby pawn" who wishes to please his fellow nobles. His later mistake with the Treasures Of Ruin is the *catastrophic event* that forces Paldea's slow reform across the next 1,500 years.
- **Recruitment ground for the Ruin Cults**, especially their Paldean merchant-class and minor-noble sympathizers.

### The Western Coalition: Kanto, Johto, Hoenn `[LOCKED]`

- **Three despotic isolationist nations**, each dominated by a single major noble family (think Tokugawa-era Japan).
- **Behind Cormoria in technology** but xenophobic and ambitious.
- **They instigated violence first** — broke early trade agreements with Cormoria, started border conflicts.
- **Kanto seized the Sevii Islands** from a Cormorian settlement there. This is one of the war's specific casus belli.
- **They unite against Cormoria** out of envy of Cormorian wealth and fear of Cormorian military strength.
- **The Kanto-Johto war** that Osrid fought in was Cormoria-defensive — defending against this coalition's aggression.

### The Long Arc — How the Modern Pokémon World Forms `[LOCKED]`

This is the *meta-design thesis* of the game's relationship to canon:

Across the next ~1,500–2,000 years:
- Cormorian meritocratic values **culturally bleed into Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn**
- The despotic dynasties are eventually replaced by the **early Pokémon League system**
- **The League's structural origin is Cormoria's Trials + Champion model** (with later influence from Kalos and Hisui)
- The modern, peaceful, trainer-friendly Pokémon world we know is the *outcome* of Cormorian values winning the long cultural war — even though Cormoria itself does not survive as a state

**Osrid loses his name, his memory, his nation. Cormoria loses everything. But the *idea* of Cormoria wins, two millennia later, in the institution of the Pokémon League itself.**

This is not spelled out in dialogue. It is the *capstone subtext* of the game, intelligible to lore-attentive players who think about what the modern Pokémon world's origins must have been.
