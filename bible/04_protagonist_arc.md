## 5. Protagonist — Osrid / Rainhawk / Veddev

### Identity in three phases `[LOCKED]`

**The name architecture — three identities, three relationships with player-choice:**

1. **Osrid (true name)** — player can rename. This is what the player types at the start. The "real" name in the sense of the prince's birth-given identity. Used in private dialogue by inner-circle characters who know the truth.
2. **Nomad Alban / Rainhawk Alban / variants (overseas cover identity)** — **canonically locked.** Player cannot change "Nomad" or "Alban." Used by all generic NPCs throughout the entire game. The cover identity was constructed by Vizier Umbra to hide Osrid in plain sight during his 10-year overseas exile.
3. **Veddev (post-cataclysm final identity)** — **canonically locked regardless of player entry.** The name Vandras gives him after finding him (memory-wiped) post-cataclysm; the name Osrid *chooses* to keep going forward **once his memory returns in Paldea** — a renunciation made in full possession of who he was, not in ignorance of it (§9 — memory arc).

**This name architecture is itself an expression of the project's thesis: identity is something we receive in compassion, not something we are given by birth or by the state. The player's chosen name matters during the journey. The cover identity is a cage the system forces on him. The name given in love (Veddev) is the name he chooses to keep.**

**Phase 1: The Prince (pre-game backstory)**
- Canonical name: **Prince Osrid**, First Prince of Cormoria
- Younger brother of Queen-Regent Rhydia (Osrid is the elder twin; Rhydia is the younger twin)
- **The reason he was sent to war:** *Not* a standard military draft. **Vizier Umbra deliberately got him out of the country** after Eldred's assassination 10 years before the story. Umbra calculated that Osrid was the next assassination target as the last male heir and that protecting Rhydia and Pierra was easier with Osrid removed from the country entirely. **War service was protective cover, not a coming-of-age path.**
- **Umbra constructed a false identity for Osrid: "Nomad Alban"** — a fictitious bastard child of House Alban (Grass-type masters, see Section 2 and Section 4). House Alban was quietly complicit in the cover, acknowledging the prince as their tolerated bastard cousin during the entire 10-year overseas service. The choice was strategic: "Nomad" is the Cormorian generic name for unwanted noble children (Section 2 — Nomad cultural designation), and *nobody investigates a Nomad's history because nobody cares about Nomads.*
- **Umbra remained in continuous clandestine contact with Osrid for the entire 10 years.** Umbra is the *only person in Cormoria* who knew Osrid was alive during the false-death disinformation campaign.
- **Osrid's combat mentor was Boran Surge** (Windshear rank, minor noble of House Surge — see Section 7). Boran trained Osrid in combat and Pokemon command, but **Boran never knew his mentee was the prince.** He believed he was training "some Cormorian Nomad" — a tolerated bastard cousin of House Alban. The corruption Boran inflicted on Osrid's spirit (the "Pokemon are purely military assets" philosophy) was therefore *incidental*, not targeted — it was simply how Boran treats every soldier under his command.
- **The Nomad-Alban cover identity made Osrid more vulnerable to the corruption.** Performing marginality so thoroughly for 10 years — being told by social cues that he was tolerated rather than valued — left him without firm self-concept to push back against Boran's worldview. **The false identity was carefully designed to be invisible, and inadvertently became spiritually receptive.** This was not Umbra's intent.
- Earned the rank of **Rainhawk** for leading humans and Pokémon to victory against the Western Coalition. Promotion criteria: killed 3+ high-priority targets, demonstrated leadership across multiple campaigns. **The empire promoted "Rainhawk Alban" not knowing he was the missing prince** — they saw a Nomad who somehow outperformed the proper noble heirs, and grudgingly granted him the rank. Darkly ironic: the meritocratic system independently confirmed the royal birthright already established, *despite* the system's prejudices against Nomads.
- Was **severely scarred** during an operation in the Sevii Islands near the end of his overseas service
- After Sevii: awarded a special **Noh-styled ceremonial Rainhawk mask** with an **eye-of-Horus pattern** on the front, given to him to cover the scarring. **The mask is recent — it dates from the Sevii incident, not from the start of his exile.** Most of his 10-year exile was lived under a *named* false identity (Nomad Alban) rather than a masked one. Umbra arranged Osrid's return to Cormoria after the Sevii incident, behind the new mask.
- The Ruin Cults spread false news of his death overseas as an early strategy to destabilize Cormoria and isolate Rhydia. **Only Umbra knew the death-rumors were lies** — and Umbra deliberately did not correct them, judging that Osrid's safety required the deception to stand.
- The player can rename him; "Osrid" is the canonical name. **The player's chosen name applies only to the true-name layer.** The cover identity names (Nomad, Alban) and the final identity name (Veddev) are locked regardless of player entry.

**The mask's multi-layered symbolism `[LOCKED]`:**

The mask is *not* a 10-year companion, but its symbolic weight is dense. It carries simultaneously:

1. **Practical wound coverage** — the Sevii scarring caused real physical damage; the mask is medically/practically functional.

2. **Identity protection on return** — Umbra arranged Osrid's homecoming behind the mask deliberately. The cult assumed Osrid was dead; revealing his face on return would have signaled his survival.

3. **Emotional crutch and safety blanket** — *this is the deepest layer.* After 10 years of constructed-identity living (Nomad Alban — a real name and history but not his own), returning home to *his actual identity* would be terrifying. The mask lets him be *visibly* hidden in a way the Nomad-Alban identity wasn't — the mask is *honestly* a barrier rather than dishonestly a substitute. **Wearing it gives him permission to be Rainhawk rather than Osrid during a period when he is not yet ready to be the prince again.**

4. **Symbol of his entire decade** — even though the mask is recent, it *crystallizes* what 10 years of false-identity living did to him. The mask is the visible expression of a habit of hiding that started long before the mask itself. **He doesn't need the mask to hide; he needs the mask because hiding has become part of who he is.**

5. **Symbol of his self-concept** — Osrid has lived as someone other than himself for so long that his "real face" feels foreign to him. The mask is the face he recognizes. *Removing the mask is partly removing the face he's used to having.*

6. **The Eye of Horus iconography** — the symbol on the mask is associated with *protection, healing, and restoration* in ancient Egyptian mythology. A mask depicting the eye of restoration *over a wounded face* is the visible metaphor for Osrid's whole journey: he is wounded, he hides the wound, but the symbol on his hiding-place is *the symbol of healing*. **The mask was right; he was being restored. He just couldn't see his own face during the process.**

7. **The scarring beneath is mild** — by endgame the physical scarring has faded to where his face just looks "a little more lined than a normal person." **The mask is not held in place by his wounds. It is held in place by his fear of being seen.** The wounds were the *occasion* for the mask, but the *reason* it stays on is psychological. His fear of being seen was always larger than the thing he was afraid of revealing.

**Phase 2: The Rainhawk (the game)**
- **Wears the empire-era Rainhawk mask constantly throughout the main game** — including when alone, when with Rhydia, when with Umbra, when with Eden. **He never removes the mask privately.** The mask comes off exactly *once* during the entire game, in the Hall of Fame unmasking sequence (described below).
- Most NPCs address him with combinations of his cover identity: **"Nomad," "Rainhawk Alban," "the new Rainhawk," "Nomad Rainhawk"** (slightly contemptuous), or **"Rainhawk"** alone (respectful military shorthand)
- Inner-circle characters who know his true identity address him by his player-chosen true name (default "Osrid")
- **For the first half of the game, only Umbra knows Osrid is the prince.** Not Rhydia. Not Pierra. Not Glaive. Even Rhydia does not know her brother is alive until ~50% through the story, when she joins Osrid for a Sword Cult encounter and the truth is revealed under combat conditions.
- **Glaive learns** Osrid's identity later — after the Rhydia reveal, when Glaive's own arc demands it
- **Silas Moray** learns last — only at the moment of the public unmasking
- The rings of trust expand outward from Umbra → Rhydia → Glaive → the larger empire post-unmasking

**The Hall of Fame Unmasking Scene — climax of Act V `[LOCKED]`:**

The culmination of Osrid's character arc. The single moment when the mask comes off.

**Sequence:**
1. Osrid defeats Rhydia in the Champion battle
2. Both enter the **Hall of Fame** — the empire-era proto-Pokemon League Hall of Fame. **Osrid's team is preserved using daguerreotype-equivalent pre-industrial photographic technology** (or a pre-photographic mechanical-magical equivalent — see Section 9). The image captures the Champion and his team at the moment of victory; the image is then placed in a vault to eternally preserve the moment. **Osrid himself is captured in the photograph still masked.** The eternal record preserves Rainhawk Alban — *the masked Champion*. After Osrid removes the mask outside, the Hall photograph still shows him in it. **Centuries later, scholars and historians studying that image will see a masked Champion and may never know the man beneath. The mask becomes a museum artifact in the same moment Osrid sheds it.**
3. Osrid leaves the Hall of Fame with Rhydia and emerges to a **massive public crowd** waiting outside
4. **Osrid removes the mask in front of the crowd.** Public, maximally visible. The crowd sees a face they don't recognize — a slightly weathered young man with mild lining around his features (the faded scarring).
5. **Rhydia announces his true identity** — speaking the name "Osrid" (or the player-chosen true name) to Cormoria. **For most of the crowd, this is the moment they realize they have been celebrating the missing prince.**
6. Rhydia names him **Regent-King and Champion of Cormoria** — transferring both her political authority and her battle authority to him. *She becomes free of the regency she didn't fully want and the Championship she earned at the cost of believing her brother was dead.*

**Why this moment lands so powerfully:**
- Osrid does not remove the mask alone, or privately, or to a single trusted figure. **He removes it to a massive public.** This is the ultimate vulnerability — not "I am ready to be seen by one person" but "I am ready to be seen by everyone."
- The crowd has been celebrating *Rainhawk Alban's* victory. Then they see his face, hear his true name, and realize they have been celebrating the *missing prince*. **This becomes the defining moment of an entire generation of Cormorians** — *"I was there when the prince returned."*
- **Rhydia is seeing her brother's adult face for the first time in 10 years.** She knew him as a 10-year-old; she sees him as a 20-year-old in the moment of his coronation. *For Rhydia, the unmasking is also a reunion with the face she remembered as a child.* The political moment and the family moment collapse into one.
- **The mild scarring is the perfect ironic detail.** Osrid built up the mask as a barrier against *something terrible* — and when he finally removes it, the terrible thing he was hiding turns out to be *just a slightly more weathered face*. His fear was disproportionate to the reality. **The mask was always heavier than the face beneath.**
- **Parallel to Glaive's heart-opening evolution.** Glaive's Shelgon refused to evolve until he opened his heart; Osrid's face refused to be shown until he opened his identity. Both characters held back by their own self-protection; both transformations happen at the same kind of internal threshold.

**Phase 3: Veddev — the chosen identity (epilogue) `[LOCKED]`**

This is the project's most distinctive narrative move and one of its boldest departures from genre conventions.

- After the sealing cataclysm (Section 10), Osrid is presumed dead by Cormoria. The empire mourns its Regent-King and Champion. Pierra and Rhydia mourn him as lost. **Cormoria does not learn that he survived.**
- He is found post-cataclysm by **Vandras Amber-Letz** (merchant, eventually direct ancestor of Volo and distant ancestor of Cynthia). **Vandras has no idea who Osrid is.** Vandras adopts him as a younger brother in pure compassion-for-a-stranger.
- Vandras gives him the name **Veddev** — a name with no political weight, no royal blessing (Vandras has no surname-bestowing authority; Veddev is not an Arceus-blessed name). It is simply the name a stranger gives in love.
- **Osrid's memory arc `[UPDATED v0.9.14]`.** The sealing cataclysm's backlash **shocks him and wipes nearly all of his memory** (§9 — the three-stone macroresonance seal). His memory is **restored later, in Paldea**, when he reunites with Eden in a Pokémon battle. By the end he again **remembers everything** — his childhood, his exile, his mentorship under Boran, his return, the Trials, the unmasking, the Champion battle, the cult crisis, Silas's death, Nemo's death, the sealing — and it is *from that recovered, memory-intact vantage* that he makes his final choice.
- **Osrid chooses to discard his old identity completely.** He chooses to accept Veddev as his name going forward. He chooses not to return to Cormoria. He chooses not to inform Pierra or Rhydia that he survived. He chooses to leave the prince-life entirely behind.

**Why Osrid chooses Veddev:**
- He set out to save his family and right the wrongs done to his father. **He did that.** The revenge — in the sense of paying back what was owed — is complete.
- He does not want to continue being defined by the wrongs that were done. He wants to live in the future the saving made possible.
- He has no desire to be a prince or rule Cormoria. **The role is finished.** He has done what he set out to do; he gets to choose what comes next.
- **A brand new name, a new family (Vandras's), and Eden — these are all he needs to move forward.**
- He is leaving the past behind for good, but keeping what is ultimately most important to him for the future.

**This is renunciation as character culmination, not loss.** Most Pokemon player-protagonists end their journeys with their identity intact. Ours ends with **the protagonist remembering everything but choosing a new name to carry forward.** Identity is something we receive in compassion (Vandras's gift of the name Veddev) and *choose to keep* — not something we are given by birth or by the state. **Osrid's choice to keep "Veddev" over "Cormoria" is a quiet theological statement: he chooses the surname-of-love over the surname-of-divinity.**

**The journey through Paldea:**
- Veddev travels east with Vandras's entourage
- Delivers the four sealed cursed objects to the treasure-loving Paldean king (the canonical Paldean sealer per SV lore — Veddev's role in canonical history is preserved)
- **During the transaction, Eden walks in.** This is the moment that completes Veddev's transformation.

**The Eden reunion — pre-recognition and the completion of renunciation `[LOCKED]`:**
- Veddev is in Paldea performing the transaction as someone who has *committed* to leaving his past behind. He has decided not to be Osrid anymore. He has folded that life away.
- **Eden walks through the door — and his commitment to forgetting collapses at exactly the place where it could not hold.**
- It is not love-at-first-sight in the conventional sense. It is **conscious memory refusing to be renounced when faced with its actual referent.** Veddev sees her, and the renunciation breaks open. *The presence of Eden completes his renunciation by giving him something better to choose than the past.*
- **Eden, for her part, recognizes him in stages.** She had loved the masked Rainhawk for the latter half of the game without ever seeing his face. She saw his face briefly at the Hall of Fame unmasking. Now she sees him again, unmasked, in Paldea — older, more weathered, presenting himself as a merchant. **She recognizes his body language first** (pre-verbal, pre-rational — she has loved this body's bearing for years), **then his face**, then accepts that this is the same man.
- **Eden chooses to call him Veddev** going forward. She does not insist on the prince-name. She is meeting him in the present, accepting the identity he has chosen, recognizing that her love does not depend on which name he carries. **Her love adapts to his new identity rather than dragging him back to his old one.**
- He keeps remembering everything else of his prior life — but **only Eden matters enough to take with him.** Cormoria continues without him. His mother continues without him. His sister continues without him. The empire he saved continues without him. *He is not coming back.* **Eden is the only thing strong enough to make him keep something from his old life.**

**The marriage and after:**
- Veddev and Eden marry in Paldea
- They build a new life together
- They may have a hand in sealing the Treasures further (working alongside the Paldean king) and establishing the proto-detective tradition
- **Their children carry the Amber-Letz family name** — Vandras's family by adoption. The Amber-Letz name evolves to **Berlitz** across ~1,800 years (Cynthia's canonical family name)
- **Vandras never learns who Veddev "really is."** The greatest fan-project Pokemon family lineage we are establishing (Berlitz → Volo → Cynthia) descends from a man whose adoptive family never knew the truth of his origin. Vandras's compassion saved the Prince of Cormoria, and Vandras dies a stranger to that fact.
- **Cormoria mourns Osrid eternally.** Pierra outlives her son's *choice not to come home*, not just her son's apparent death in the sealing. Rhydia lives the rest of her reign believing she lost him twice — first to the false news 10 years earlier, then for real in the cataclysm. **Neither learns the truth.** This is *another sacrifice* Osrid makes — he doesn't get reunion with his family in exchange for being free of them.

### Character arc `[LOCKED]`

Osrid's arc has *multiple* layered emotional axes; this is one of the most psychologically detailed protagonists in fan-project Pokemon. Each axis develops in parallel across the game and culminates with renunciation:

**The "Pokémon as partners" axis (the project's central thesis):**
- **Begin:** Distrustful of grand causes; commanded Pokémon as a soldier but never truly bonded with one. **Has internalized Boran Surge's "Pokemon as weapons" philosophy** through nearly a decade of mentorship. This is not Osrid's natural disposition — he was a kind child before the war — but it is what his combat training inculcated.
- **Inciting moment:** Vizier Umbra gives him a *true partner Pokémon* — one of three unique starters (Frigibax, Teddiursa, Tinkatink), hand-selected for the prince's role. **Umbra's gift is the deliberate beginning of Osrid's re-learning of partnership** — bookending the Boran-Surge corruption with a renewed thesis.
- **Middle:** Investigates cult activity. Develops emotional bonds with his team that contrast with his military command-and-control mindset. Notable beats include the Crabominable adoption scene (Section 8 — Baradus profile, Section 11 — Principle 1+2 Exemplar), where the player can *consciously choose* compassion against Osrid's 10 years of Boran-Surge conditioning.
- **Late game:** Re-internalizes partnership through cumulative choices and the unmasking moment.
- **Climax:** Wields partnership at cosmic scale in the sealing ritual, with Ting-Lu (Nemo's gift) and his own party Pokemon.

**The identity-concealment trauma axis:**
- **Background:** Osrid has been hiding his identity for *10 years*, since he was 10 years old. **Hiding is his normal state.** Lived as Nomad Alban overseas. Returned home behind a mask he was given mid-Sevii operation.
- **Internal conflict throughout the game:** Should he reveal himself and reclaim his royal identity? Or should he complete the Trials *as Rainhawk* and quietly disappear, seeking a new life elsewhere? **The idea of unmasking himself and letting everyone know he's alive terrifies him** because he's been hidden for the entirety of his self-conscious life.
- **This is a core emotional beat of the whole story**, alongside the partnership thesis.
- **Unmasking is a real sacrifice**, not a triumph. He is not "claiming his rightful identity" but *giving up the safety of anonymity* that he has *needed* for a decade. The Hall of Fame unmasking happens because he *has to* (to save the empire, to confront the cults, to face Silas, to be Champion + Regent-King). Not because he wants to.

**The renunciation arc — the bravest narrative move:**
- Osrid sacrifices his hidden-Rainhawk identity to become public Prince/Champion/Regent-King. He does the unmasking he had been terrified of. He pays the cost of being known.
- He survives the sealing cataclysm (memory intact). **He chooses to leave the prince-identity behind anyway.** Not because he was forced to, but because he had already done what he set out to do.
- **Veddev in Paldea is what Osrid originally wanted to be:** someone with a peaceful life, no royal expectations, no need to be the prince. *He chooses this life consciously, with his memory intact.* **He is not running away. He is choosing forward.**
- The man who had every reason to be a prince and chose to be a merchant instead. **The man whose identity is not what he was given by birth but what he chose by love.**

**Eden as the only kept piece of the past:**
- Eden is the one thing Osrid is *unwilling* to leave behind. Everything else — Cormoria, his mother, his sister, the empire he saved, the royal birthright, the very name "Osrid" — he leaves. Eden he keeps.
- **She becomes the continuation of him**, not the keeper of his old self. Their marriage is between Veddev and Eden, not between Osrid-with-Eden.

### The double irony of Osrid's Rainhawk rank `[LOCKED]`

There is significant irony in the fact that Osrid rose through the military ranks on his own merits, but it turns out **he is the highest-ranking noble all along and never needed the prestige.** The empire promoted him to Rainhawk seeing a Nomad who somehow outperformed the proper noble heirs; he was, unbeknownst to them, the missing prince to whom the empire owed everything. **The meritocratic system independently confirmed the royal birthright already established**, *despite* the system's prejudices against Nomads — a quiet validation of Cormoria's deepest political principle (merit reflects truth) layered with darkly ironic dramatic structure (the system's confirmation was never needed).

And the final irony: **after all his identities have been confirmed — Nomad, Rainhawk, Prince, Champion, Regent-King — he discards them all in favor of Veddev, the name a merchant gave him in compassion.** *None of his earned or inherited names were what he chose to keep.*

**The twin-mirror dynamic with Rhydia `[LOCKED]`:**

Osrid and Rhydia are twins who responded to the same family trauma (their father Eldred's assassination at age 10) in *opposite* ways. **Both responses are coping mechanisms for the same wound, but they pull in opposite directions.**

- **Osrid went inward.** He was sent overseas under a false identity (Nomad Alban), then returned behind a Rainhawk mask. He has spent 10 years *hidden*. His instinct under pressure is to disappear, to conceal, to occupy a smaller and smaller visible self. The mask is the literal embodiment of this instinct.
- **Rhydia went outward.** She stayed in Cormoria, took over her mother's regency in all but formal title, became the Champion, and made herself the most publicly visible figure in the empire. She does the work of state on the front lines, mingling with the people, being seen constantly. Her popular legitimacy is *earned through visibility*, not granted by ceremony.

**The twins are mirror images of each other in their relationship to identity.** Osrid is the prince who refuses to be seen; Rhydia is the princess who makes herself constantly visible. Each is incomplete without the other.

When they reunite at ~50% (Rhydia learns Osrid is the new Rainhawk), **they begin in some sense learning from each other** about how to inhabit royal identity. Osrid may teach her something about the cost of visibility (he has paid that cost in shadow form for a decade); Rhydia may teach him something about the courage of being seen. By the Hall of Fame unmasking moment at the climax of Act V, **Osrid has integrated Rhydia's lesson** — he has become visible, claimed his identity publicly. By the renunciation epilogue, **he has *also* completed his own arc** — he chooses, having been visible, to disappear again, this time on his own terms rather than to protect himself.

Rhydia continues as Champion after Osrid disappears — but **she carries forward something of his integrated learning.** She has seen what it cost him to be visible; she may govern with more compassion for those who choose privacy as a result.
