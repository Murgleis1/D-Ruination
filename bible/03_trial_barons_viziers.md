## 4. The Cormorian Empire (Internal Structure)

### Political structure `[LOCKED]`

- **Imperial court structure.** The Empire is ruled by a monarch with a tiered support structure of Viziers and Trial Houses.
- **Currently ruled by Queen-Regent Rhydia** — the protagonist's younger sister.
- **Court hierarchy:** Queen-Regent → 4 Viziers (one of whom is Umbra) → 9 noble Trial Houses → military officer corps → commoners.
- `[OPEN]` Who is the absent monarch Rhydia is regent for? Where is Cormoria's geographic capital?

### Military culture `[LOCKED]`

- Empire maintains a formal military with **rank-specific ceremonial masks** for decorated officers
- **Rank hierarchy** (the full structure is documented in **Section 2 — Cormorian military rank hierarchy**):
  - **Cadet → Lieutenant → Strategos → Windshear → Rainhawk → Tarasque → Dawnstar → Vizier**
  - First three ranks (Cadet, Lieutenant, Strategos) constitute ~90% of military personnel
  - **Rainhawk** is the watershed rank — empire promotes to Rainhawk only those they believe could eventually lead a Trial, head a guild, or be knighted into nobility
- The protagonist earned his **Rainhawk** mask during the Kanto-Johto-Hoenn war. Mask is a distinctive Noh-styled ceremonial design with an eye-of-Horus pattern on the front, awarded to him after he was severely scarred during a Sevii Islands operation (see Section 5).
- Nemo Korolev rose through every tier from Cadet to **Dawnstar** by merit alone, faster than any officer in Cormorian history — including Eldred. He then voluntarily stepped down to the 9th Trial Baron seat (see Section 7 — Nemo profile).
- **The Champion is usually a former Vizier, a Tarasque or Dawnstar, or a particularly prodigal royal.** New Champions emerge once in a few generations.

Each Trial Baron specializes in a single monotype — the type identity is hereditary, passed down across generations within the noble house, *except* for Nemo Korolev (the 9th Trial Baron) who is the only non-noble in the group.

### Templar Arceus Order `[LOCKED]`

A religious-civil order present in every Cormorian city. Templars serve Arceus directly and maintain civic infrastructure on Arceus's behalf. Key functions:

- **Operate the Tavern network** (see below)
- **Provide the Braviary inter-city travel service** (Section 9)
- **Maintain religious-civil order** as Arceus's intermediaries in the empire
- **Operate as informal intelligence-gatherers** (their cross-city network is excellent for noticing political and cult activity)

**Internal division:** The Templar Arceus order is **internally divided.** While the majority of Templars are doctrinally and personally opposed to the Ruin Cults (which they view as heretical anti-Arceus), **some Templars are secretly cult-aligned** — typically Silas-Moray-influenced or Baradus-bought. This creates an ongoing tension: the order *should* be a natural enemy of the cults, but its corruption limits its effectiveness.

**Templar Pokemon partnerships:** Templars typically carry **Braviary** (Normal/Flying, the *honest civic service* form). Silas Moray, in contrast, carries a **shiny Hisuian Braviary named Thauma** (Psychic/Flying — the manipulator's form). The fact that Silas's national bird is the *aberrant* form is itself a worldbuilding statement: the corrupted Templar institution has produced its own corrupted patron.

**Leadership — the Archbishop `[LOCKED v0.9.7]`:** The Templar Arceus order is led by **Archbishop Ambrose Caymen**, a Steel-type master and one of the project's most thematically complex characters. He sits at the top of the order's *warrior-priest* tradition (the militant side of Arceus's service, alongside the civic-administration side). His childhood friendship with Nemo Korolev and his cult-pressured indecision through the early-to-mid game culminate in a player fight that resolves his moral paralysis through partnership-demonstration. See Section 7 — Ambrose Caymen for the full character entry.

### Taverns — Empire-era Pokémon Centers `[LOCKED]`

In our era, **Taverns** are the empire-era equivalent of modern Pokémon Centers, but they serve *both* people *and* Pokémon. Each city has at least one Tavern, typically operated by a Templar Arceus presence.

**Functions:**
- **Heal Pokémon** to full (same as Pokémon Center function)
- **Issue a HealPass** after a full heal (carrying forward Dreamstone Mysteries' HealPass mechanic — see Section 11 Principle 4)
- **Lodge people** — players can pay (or earn through quest completion) overnight lodging, which advances day-to-night time progression
- **Social gathering space** — NPCs gather at Taverns. Many living-world dialogue beats happen here (Principle 1)
- **Source of Braviary travel** — the local Templar at each Tavern has a Braviary partner that the player can pay to ride between cities (Section 9)

**Design implication:** Taverns make cities feel *bigger and more lived-in* than modern Pokémon Centers do in DM. A Tavern is a *place to be* (drink, eat, gather information, sleep, meet NPCs) as well as a place to heal Pokémon. Each Tavern is a worldbuilding opportunity.

### The political crisis the cults have created `[LOCKED]`

The 9 Trial Houses are **divided in their loyalties**. Some remain loyal to the royal family. Others have been **infiltrated, corrupted, or actively recruited** by the Sword Cult (the dominant active cult; see Section 8). Silas Moray, as Vizier of Commerce and secret cult 2IC, has facilitated much of the corruption through psychic manipulation and intelligence-network co-option.

Part of the political intrigue of the game is **discovering which families are loyal and which have been turned**. The player must navigate this as they progress through the Trials. The corruption is widespread enough that **the empire's official intelligence apparatus is unreliable** — Nemo Korolev specifically built a *private mercenary intelligence network* that operates outside the corrupted official channels, because he could not trust the state apparatus to be uncompromised.
