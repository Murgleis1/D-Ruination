### Hard canon constraints (cannot contradict)

- The Treasures of Ruin originated in an unnamed eastern land (Cormoria, in our setting). `[LOCKED]`
- A merchant from the east delivered them to a treasure-loving Paldean king during the early Paldean Empire era. `[LOCKED]`
- The four objects are a sword (Chien-Pao), tablets (Wo-Chien), a vessel (Ting-Lu), and beads (Chi-Yu). `[LOCKED]`
- The objects were born from hatred, grudge, fear, and envy respectively. `[LOCKED]`
- After delivery, the objects awoke, destroyed the king's castle, and were sealed in four Paldean shrines. `[LOCKED]`
- Calyrex is a benevolent grass/psychic king of ancient Galar with healing and telepathic powers. `[LOCKED]`
- Slither Wing is a Paradox Pokémon — the prehistoric ancestor of Volcarona. Encountering one in Ancient Cormoria is canon-compatible. `[LOCKED]`
- The Paldean Empire was founded ~2,000 years before modern. Our game sits in its early, consolidating era. `[LOCKED]`

### Soft canon connections (lore-additive, can stay implicit)

- Vandras Amber-Letz's adopted family produces Volo (PLA) and Cynthia (Gen 4). Family name Amber-Letz → Berlitz via linguistic drift. `[LOCKED]`
- Glaive founds Blackthorn City and the Dragon's Den order. Lance's family descends from that order. Glaive's nickname is *Blackthorn*. `[LOCKED — soft canon]`
- Umbra is the ancestor of Professor Tenebris from Dreamstone Mysteries. Latin-shadow naming tradition. `[LOCKED]`
- Cult remnants eventually become Team Somber (Dreamstone Mysteries villains). `[LOCKED]`
- Cult-defectors who turned to opposition eventually establish the detective academy (Dreamstone Mysteries). `[LOCKED]`
- Eden may have a hand in establishing the proto-detective tradition alongside Veddev/Osrid after Paldean sealing — *implied through her temperament and choices, not stated as canon*. `[LOCKED — implied]`

### Canon humility principle

The Ruin Cults' beliefs about Calyrex and Arceus are *their* beliefs, presented through their texts and rhetoric. Canon does not confirm Calyrex can mind-control all Pokémon. The cults' fanaticism is built on a *plausible misreading* of theology, not literal mechanical truth. `[LOCKED]`

### Thematic thesis `[LOCKED]`

**Pokémon are partners, not weapons. Anything you treat as a weapon eventually becomes a Treasure of Ruin.**

This thesis is *demonstrated* through character arcs (especially Glaive and Osrid), not preached through dialogue. The Treasures of Ruin are the anti-Pokémon — beings born from human emotions who can only be wielded or sealed, never bonded with. Every major character arc refracts this thesis:

- Osrid: soldier-who-commanded → trainer-who-bonds → man-who-chooses-his-own-name-over-his-birth-name
- Glaive: vengeance-driven weapon-trainer → opens his heart → his Shelgon evolves into Salamence
- Eden: thief who *steals* partners → woman who helps *seal* the things that can't be partnered with
- Vandras: merchant of objects → *adopts a person*, recognizing a lost soul is worth more than any object

Note one design tension we hold deliberately: the starter Pokémon are weapons-coded (Baxcalibur, Ursaluna, Tinkaton). The prince begins his trainer journey with creatures that *look* like weapons and must learn they aren't. This is the thesis in microcosm.
